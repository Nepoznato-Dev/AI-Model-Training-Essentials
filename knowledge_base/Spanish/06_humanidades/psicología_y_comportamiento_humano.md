# Psicología y Comportamiento humano

## Introducción to Psicología

Psicología is el scientific study de mind y behavior. It examines how people think, feel, act, y interact. Major perspectives en Psicología incluyen biological (examining brain structures y neurotransmitters), behavioral (focusing on observable behaviors y learning), cognitive (studying mental processes like memory y decision-making), psychodynamic (exploring unconscious drives y conflicts, originating con Freud), humanistic (emphasizing personal growth y self-actualization), y social-cultural (examining how environment y Cultura shape behavior). Psicología uses scientific methods including experiments, observations, surveys, y case studies to understand human experience.

## Cognitive Psicología

Cognitive Psicología studies mental processes including attention, perception, memory, Idioma, problem-solving, y decision-making. Attention is el ability to focus on specific stimuli while filtering out others. Perception involves organizing y interpreting sensory information. Memory has three main stages: encoding (getting information en), storage (retaining information), y retrieval (getting information out). Short-term memory holds limited information briefly; long-term memory has vast capacity para extended periods. Working memory temporarily manipulates information para complex tasks. Idioma acquisition involves understanding y producing Comunicación. Problem-solving strategies incluyen algorithms (step-by-step procedures) y heuristics (mental shortcuts). Decision-making can be influenced by biases y emotions.

## Developmental Psicología

Developmental Psicología examines how people change y grow throughout their lifespan. Prenatal Desarrollo incluyens germinal, embryonic, y fetal stages. Infancy (0-2 years) features rapid physical growth, attachment formation, y sensorimotor learning. Childhood (2-12 years) involves Idioma Desarrollo, social skills, y concrete thinking. Adolescence (12-18 years) brings puberty, identity formation, y abstract razóning. Early adulthood (20s-30s) se centra en career, relationships, y independence. Middle adulthood (40s-60s) often involves career consolidation y caring para aging parents. Late adulthood (65+) may incluyen retirement, health challenges, y reflection. Key theories incluyen Piaget's stages de cognitive Desarrollo, Erikson's psychosocial stages, y Kohlberg's moral Desarrollo theory. Naturaleza (genetics) y nurture (environment) both influence Desarrollo.

## Social Psicología

Social Psicología studies how individuals think, feel, y behave en social contexts. Conformity is adjusting behavior to match group norms, demonstrated en Asch's line experiments. Obedience is complying con authority figures, studied en Milgram's shock experiments. Social facilitation improves Rendimiento on simple tasks when others watch. Social loafing reduces effort en groups. Groupthink occurs when groups prioritize harmony over critical evaluation. Prejudice is preconceived judgment about groups; discrimination is unequal treatment. Stereotypes are generalized beliefs about groups. Attribution theory explains how we interpret others' behavior (internal vs. external causes). el fundamental attribution error overestimates personality y underestimates situation when judging others. Bystander effect reduces helping behavior when others are present. Aggression can be influenced by biology, frustration, y social learning. Attraction involves proximity, similarity, physical attractiveness, y reciprocity.

## Personality Psicología

Personality refers to enduring patterns de thoughts, feelings, y behaviors. el Big Five personality traits are openness (curiosity, creativity), conscientiousness (organization, dependability), extraversion (sociability, energy from others), agreeableness (cooperation, compassion), y neuroticism (emotional instability, anxiety). Freud's psychoanalytic theory proposed id (instincts), ego (reality), y superego (morality). Humanistic theories (Maslow, Rogers) emphasize growth potential y self-actualization. Trait theories identify consistent characteristics across situations. Type theories categorize people (e.g., Type A vs. Type B behavior patterns). Personality develops through genetics, environment, y experiences. Assessment tools incluyen self-report inventories (MMPI) y projective tests (Rorschach inkblots).

## Mental Health y Disorders

Mental health encompasses emotional, psychological, y social well-being. Psychological disorders are patterns causing distress or impaired functioning. Anxiety disorders incluyen generalized anxiety, panic disorder, phobias, y social anxiety. Mood disorders incluyen depression (persistent sadness) y bipolar disorder (mood swings). Schizophrenia involves distorted thinking, perceptions, y emotions. Eating disorders incluyen anorexia nervosa, bulimia, y binge-eating. Personality disorders involve inflexible patterns causing problems (e.g., borderline, antisocial). Trauma-related disorders incluyen PTSD following traumatic Eventos. el DSM-5 (Diagnostic y Statistical Manual) classifies mental disorders. Causes de disorders involve biological factors (genetics, brain chemistry), psychological factors (trauma, learned patterns), y social factors (stress, environment). Stigma surrounding mental illness can prevent people from seeking help.

## Therapeutic Approaches

Psychotherapy involves treating mental disorders through psychological methods. Psychodynamic therapy explores unconscious conflicts y past experiences. Cognitive-behavioral therapy (CBT) changes negative thought patterns y behaviors. Humanistic therapy emphasizes self-exploration y personal growth. Behavioral therapy modifies behaviors through conditioning techniques. Family therapy addresses family dynamics affecting individuals. Group therapy provides peer Soporte. Biomedical therapies incluyen medications (antidepressants, antipsychotics, mood stabilizers) y procedures (ECT, TMS). Evidence-based practice integrates research evidence, clinical expertise, y patient values. Prevention y early intervention improve outcomes. Self-care practices incluyen exercise, sleep, Nutrición, mindfulness, y social connection.

## Neuroscience y Behavior

Neuroscience examines el nervous system's role en behavior. Neurons are nerve cells transmitting information through electrical y chemical signals. Neurotransmitters are chemicals transmitting signals between neurons (dopamine, serotonin, norepinephrine, GABA, glutamate). el central nervous system incluyens brain y spinal cord. el peripheral nervous system connects CNS to body. el brain's cerebrum handles higher functions; cerebellum coordinates movement; brainstem controls vital functions. el limbic system processes emotions y memory (amygdala, hippocampus, hypothalamus). Brain plasticity allows neural connections to change con experience. Neuroimaging techniques (MRI, fMRI, PET scans) visualize brain structure y activity. Hormones from el endocrine system also influence behavior y emotions.

## Motivation y Emotion

Motivation drives behavior toward goals. Intrinsic motivation comes from internal satisfaction; extrinsic motivation from external rewards. Maslow's hierarchy proposes needs from physiological to self-actualization. Drive-reduction theory suggests behavior aims to reduce internal tension. Instinct theory proposes innate behavioral patterns. Emotion involves physiological arousal, expressive behaviors, y conscious experience. Major emotion theories incluyen James-Lange (emotion follows physiological response), Cannon-Bard (emotion y response occur simultaneously), y Schachter-Singer (emotion depends on interpretation de arousal). Basic emotions incluyen happiness, sadness, anger, fear, surprise, y disgust. Emotional intelligence involves recognizing y managing emotions en oneself y others. Stress triggers el fight-or-flight response; chronic stress harms health.

## Learning y Conditioning

Learning is relatively permanent behavior change through experience. Classical conditioning (Pavlov) pairs neutral stimuli con natural responses. Operant conditioning (Skinner) uses consequences to shape behavior. Positive reinforcement adds desirable stimuli to increase behavior; negative reinforcement removes aversive stimuli to increase behavior. Punishment decreases behavior. Observational learning (Bandura) occurs by watching others. Latent learning happens without immediate reinforcement. Insight learning involves sudden problem comprehension. Habituation reduces response to repeated stimuli. Generalization responds similarly to similar stimuli; discrimination distinguishes between stimuli. Extinction weakens conditioned responses when reinforcement stops.
