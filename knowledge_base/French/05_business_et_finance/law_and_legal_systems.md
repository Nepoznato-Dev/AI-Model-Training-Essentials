# Droit et systèmes juridiques

## Legal Systems Overview

A legal system is the framework of rules, institutions, and procedures that govern a society. The main legal systems worldwide include common law (based on judicial precedents, used in UK, US, Canada, Australia), civil law (based on codified statutes, used in most of Europe, Latin America, Japan), religious law (based on religious texts, such as Islamic Sharia law), and customary law (based on traditional practices). Common law systems rely heavily on case law and the principle of stare decisis (precedent must be followed). Civil law systems use comprehensive legal codes that judges apply to cases.

## Branches of Law

Law is typically divided into public law and private law. Public law governs relationships between individuals and the state, including constitutional law, administrative law, and criminal law. Private law (civil law) governs relationships between individuals, including contract law, tort law, property law, and family law. Criminal law deals with offenses against society punishable by the state. Civil law addresses disputes between parties where remedies typically involve compensation rather than punishment.

## Constitutional Law

Constitutional law concerns the interpretation and implementation of a country's constitution. A constitution is the supreme law establishing the structure of government, distribution of powers, and fundamental rights. Judicial review allows courts to determine whether laws or government actions violate the constitution. The separation of powers divides government into legislative (makes laws), executive (enforces laws), and judicial (interprets laws) branches. Checks and balances prevent any one branch from becoming too powerful. Fundamental rights often include freedom of speech, religion, assembly, due process, and equal protection under the law.

## Contract Law

A contract is a legally binding agreement between two or more parties. Essential elements of a valid contract include offer (a clear proposal), acceptance (agreement to the terms), consideration (something of value exchanged), capacity (legal ability to contract), and legality (purpose must be legal). Breach of contract occurs when a party fails to fulfill obligations. Remedies for breach include damages (monetary compensation), specific performance (court orders fulfillment), or cancellation. Contracts can be written, oral, or implied by conduct, though certain types must be in writing under the Statute of Frauds.

## Tort Law

Tort law addresses civil wrongs that cause harm or loss, resulting in legal liability. Negligence is the failure to exercise reasonable care, causing injury to another. To prove negligence, a plaintiff must show: duty of care existed, breach of that duty, causation, and damages. Intentional torts include assault, battery, false imprisonment, defamation (libel and slander), and trespass. Strict liability applies regardless of fault in certain cases (defective products, dangerous activities). Damages in tort cases compensate for medical expenses, lost wages, pain and suffering, and sometimes punitive damages to punish egregious conduct.

## Property Law

Property law governs ownership and use of real property (land and buildings) and personal property (movable items). Real property rights include possession, control, exclusion, enjoyment, and disposition. Types of ownership include fee simple (absolute ownership), life estate (ownership for lifetime), and leasehold (temporary possession). Landlord-tenant law regulates rental agreements. Intellectual property is a form of intangible property protecting creations of the mind. Eminent domain allows governments to take private property for public use with just compensation. Zoning laws regulate land use in specific areas.

## Criminal Law and Procedure

Criminal law defines crimes and punishments. Crimes are classified as felonies (serious offenses punishable by imprisonment over one year) or misdemeanors (less serious offenses). Elements of a crime typically include actus reus (guilty act) and mens rea (guilty mind/intent). The presumption of innocence means defendants are innocent until proven guilty beyond a reasonable doubt. Constitutional protections in criminal proceedings include protection against unreasonable searches and seizures, right to counsel, right to a speedy and public trial, protection against self-incrimination, and protection against double jeopardy. Sentencing may involve imprisonment, fines, probation, or capital punishment (in some jurisdictions).

## Family Law

Family law deals with domestic relations and family matters. Marriage is a legal union creating rights and obligations between spouses. Divorce (dissolution of marriage) involves division of assets, spousal support (alimony), and child custody. Child custody determines where children live and who makes decisions about their welfare. Child support is financial support paid by non-custodial parents. Adoption creates a legal parent-child relationship. Domestic violence laws protect family members from abuse. Paternity establishes legal fatherhood. Prenuptial agreements specify asset division if marriage ends.

## Intellectual Property

Intellectual property (IP) protects creations of the mind. Patents grant inventors exclusive rights to make, use, or sell inventions for a limited period (typically 20 years). Copyright protects original works of authorship (books, music, art, software) for the author's life plus 70 years in most countries. Trademarks protect brand identifiers (names, logos, slogans) used in commerce. Trade secrets protect confidential business information providing competitive advantage. IP infringement occurs when someone uses protected material without permission. Fair use doctrines allow limited use of copyrighted material without permission for purposes like criticism, education, or parody.

## Employment and Labor Law

Employment law governs relationships between employers and employees. At-will employment allows termination by either party at any time (common in the US). Employment contracts specify terms of employment. Minimum wage laws set the lowest legal hourly pay. Anti-discrimination laws prohibit workplace discrimination based on race, gender, age, religion, disability, or other protected characteristics. Workers' compensation provides benefits for work-related injuries. Occupational safety regulations (like OSHA in the US) ensure safe working conditions. Labor unions represent workers in collective bargaining with employers. Wrongful termination occurs when firing violates law or public policy.

## International Law

International law governs relations between nations and international entities. Public international law includes treaties, customs, and principles binding on states. The United Nations Charter is a foundational treaty establishing the UN system. International humanitarian law (laws of war) regulates armed conflict and protects non-combatants. Human rights law establishes universal rights inherent to all humans. International criminal law prosecutes genocide, war crimes, crimes against humanity. Private international law (conflict of laws) determines which jurisdiction's laws apply in cross-border disputes. International trade law regulates commerce between nations through organizations like the WTO.

## Legal Process and Courts

Civil procedure governs how civil lawsuits are conducted. A lawsuit begins when a plaintiff files a complaint. The defendant responds with an answer. Discovery is the pre-trial exchange of information. Motions are requests for court rulings. Trial involves presenting evidence and arguments to a judge or jury. Verdict is the decision; judgment is the court's final ruling. Appeals challenge lower court decisions to higher courts. Alternative dispute resolution includes mediation (neutral facilitator helps parties reach agreement) and arbitration (neutral third party makes binding decision). Class action lawsuits allow groups with similar claims to sue collectively.

## Legal Ethics and Professional Responsibility

Legal ethics govern lawyer conduct. Attorneys must maintain client confidentiality (attorney-client privilege). Conflicts of interest must be avoided or disclosed. Lawyers owe duties of competence, diligence, and loyalty to clients. Pro bono service involves providing free legal assistance to those unable to pay. Bar associations regulate lawyer licensing and discipline. Attorney misconduct can result in sanctions, suspension, or disbarment. The attorney-client privilege protects communications between lawyers and clients from disclosure. Work product doctrine protects materials prepared in anticipation of litigation.
