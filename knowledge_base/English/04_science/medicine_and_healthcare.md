# Medicine and Healthcare Systems

## Medical Specialties

Medicine encompasses numerous specialized fields. Internal medicine focuses on adult diseases and organ systems. Cardiology deals with heart and cardiovascular conditions. Neurology addresses disorders of the nervous system. Gastroenterology covers digestive system diseases. Pulmonology treats respiratory conditions. Endocrinology manages hormonal disorders including diabetes. Nephrology focuses on kidney diseases. Oncology diagnoses and treats cancer. Hematology studies blood disorders. Rheumatology handles autoimmune and musculoskeletal conditions. Infectious disease specialists treat infections. Dermatology addresses skin conditions. Psychiatry manages mental health disorders. Pediatrics cares for children's health. Geriatrics focuses on elderly care. Emergency medicine handles acute illnesses and injuries. Surgery includes general surgery and subspecialties like orthopedic, neurosurgery, cardiothoracic, plastic, and urologic surgery. Radiology uses imaging for diagnosis and treatment. Anesthesiology manages pain and sedation during procedures. Pathology studies disease causes through tissue and lab analysis. Family medicine provides comprehensive primary care. Obstetrics and gynecology covers women's reproductive health. Ophthalmology treats eye conditions. Otolaryngology (ENT) addresses ear, nose, and throat disorders.

## Healthcare Systems

Healthcare systems vary globally. Single-payer systems (UK's NHS, Canada) have government-funded universal coverage. Multi-payer systems (US, Germany) involve multiple insurance providers. Social health insurance models (France, Japan) mandate contributions from employers and employees. Out-of-pocket systems dominate in low-income countries. Primary care serves as the first contact point, coordinating ongoing care. Secondary care involves specialist consultations and hospital services. Tertiary care provides highly specialized treatments at major centers. Preventive care aims to prevent illness through screening, vaccination, and lifestyle counseling. Urgent care handles non-emergency immediate needs. Telemedicine delivers remote healthcare via technology. Electronic health records (EHRs) store patient information digitally. Health insurance covers medical costs through premiums, deductibles, copayments, and coinsurance. Universal healthcare ensures all citizens have access to needed services.

## Pharmacology and Medications

Pharmacology studies drug actions and effects. Prescription medications require doctor authorization. Over-the-counter (OTC) drugs are available without prescription. Generic drugs contain same active ingredients as brand names at lower cost. Drug classes include antibiotics (fight bacterial infections), antivirals (treat viral infections), analgesics (pain relievers), anti-inflammatories (reduce inflammation), antihypertensives (lower blood pressure), statins (lower cholesterol), anticoagulants (prevent blood clots), antidepressants (treat depression), antipsychotics (manage psychosis), bronchodilators (open airways), insulin (manages diabetes), and chemotherapy agents (treat cancer). Pharmacokinetics examines drug absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion. Drug interactions occur when medications affect each other. Side effects are unintended drug reactions. Allergic reactions are immune responses to medications. Dosage refers to amount and frequency of administration. Clinical trials test drug safety and efficacy before approval. The FDA (US) and EMA (Europe) regulate pharmaceuticals.

## Medical Ethics

Medical ethics guides healthcare decision-making. Autonomy respects patients' right to make informed decisions. Beneficence requires acting in patients' best interests. Non-maleficence means "do no harm." Justice demands fair resource distribution. Informed consent ensures patients understand risks and benefits before procedures. Confidentiality protects patient privacy (HIPAA in US). Truth-telling requires honest communication with patients. End-of-life care raises questions about life support, palliative care, and euthanasia. Resource allocation involves difficult decisions about scarce treatments. Conflicts of interest must be disclosed and managed. Research ethics protects human subjects through institutional review boards (IRBs). Cultural competence respects diverse beliefs and practices. Professional boundaries maintain appropriate provider-patient relationships.

## Public Health

Public health focuses on population-level health promotion and disease prevention. Epidemiology studies disease patterns and causes. Vaccination programs prevent infectious diseases. Health education promotes healthy behaviors. Environmental health addresses hazards in surroundings. Occupational health protects workers. Nutrition programs combat malnutrition. Maternal and child health services support families. Injury prevention reduces accidents. Chronic disease management addresses long-term conditions. Health policy shapes healthcare delivery and access. Global health tackles international health challenges. Pandemic preparedness plans for disease outbreaks. Contact tracing identifies exposed individuals during outbreaks. Quarantine isolates potentially infected people. Herd immunity occurs when enough people are immune to protect the vulnerable. Social determinants of health include income, education, housing, and environment affecting health outcomes. Health disparities are unequal health outcomes across populations.

## Diagnostic Methods

Diagnosis identifies diseases through various methods. Physical examination assesses visible and palpable signs. Medical history gathers symptom and background information. Laboratory tests analyze blood, urine, and other samples. Complete blood count (CBC) measures blood cells. Metabolic panels assess organ function. Imaging includes X-rays (bone and dense tissue), CT scans (detailed cross-sections), MRI (soft tissue detail), ultrasound (sound waves), PET scans (metabolic activity), and mammography (breast imaging). Biopsy removes tissue for pathological examination. Endoscopy uses cameras to view internal organs. Electrocardiogram (ECG/EKG) records heart electrical activity. Electroencephalogram (EEG) measures brain waves. Spirometry tests lung function. Genetic testing identifies inherited conditions. Screening tests detect diseases before symptoms appear. Differential diagnosis lists possible conditions explaining symptoms.

## Treatment Approaches

Treatment strategies vary by condition. Pharmacotherapy uses medications. Surgery physically repairs or removes diseased tissue. Radiation therapy kills cancer cells with high-energy rays. Immunotherapy harnesses the immune system against disease. Physical therapy restores movement and function. Occupational therapy helps daily living skills. Speech therapy addresses communication disorders. Psychotherapy treats mental health through talk therapy. Lifestyle modifications include diet, exercise, and stress management. Alternative medicine includes acupuncture, herbal remedies, and chiropractic care (evidence varies). Palliative care focuses on symptom relief and quality of life. Rehabilitation helps recovery after illness or injury. Gene therapy modifies genes to treat disease. Stem cell transplantation replaces damaged cells. Dialysis filters blood when kidneys fail. Transplantation replaces failing organs. Clinical guidelines provide evidence-based treatment recommendations.

## Anatomy and Physiology Basics

Anatomy studies body structure. Physiology examines how body systems function. The integumentary system (skin, hair, nails) protects and regulates temperature. The skeletal system (206 bones) provides structure and protection. The muscular system enables movement. The nervous system (brain, spinal cord, nerves) controls body functions. The endocrine system produces hormones regulating metabolism and growth. The cardiovascular system (heart, blood vessels) circulates blood. The lymphatic system fights infection and drains fluids. The respiratory system (lungs, airways) exchanges gases. The digestive system processes food and absorbs nutrients. The urinary system (kidneys, bladder) eliminates waste. The reproductive system enables reproduction. The immune system defends against pathogens. Homeostasis maintains stable internal conditions. Cells are the basic units of life. Tissues group similar cells. Organs perform specific functions. Organ systems work together for survival.

## Common Medical Conditions

Cardiovascular diseases include hypertension (high blood pressure), coronary artery disease, heart attack, stroke, and heart failure. Respiratory conditions include asthma, COPD, pneumonia, and lung cancer. Digestive disorders include GERD, ulcers, inflammatory bowel disease, and liver disease. Endocrine disorders include diabetes (types 1 and 2), thyroid disease, and obesity. Neurological conditions include Alzheimer's disease, Parkinson's disease, epilepsy, migraines, and multiple sclerosis. Mental health conditions include depression, anxiety disorders, bipolar disorder, schizophrenia, and PTSD. Musculoskeletal issues include arthritis, osteoporosis, back pain, and fractures. Cancer affects various organs with different prognoses. Infectious diseases range from common cold to tuberculosis, HIV/AIDS, and emerging pathogens. Autoimmune diseases include lupus, rheumatoid arthritis, and type 1 diabetes. Kidney disease ranges from acute injury to chronic failure. Skin conditions include eczema, psoriasis, acne, and skin cancer.

## Health Promotion and Prevention

Preventive healthcare reduces disease risk. Regular check-ups monitor health status. Screenings detect early disease (mammograms, colonoscopies, Pap smears, PSA tests). Vaccinations prevent infectious diseases (flu, measles, HPV, COVID-19). Healthy diet emphasizes fruits, vegetables, whole grains, lean proteins, and limits processed foods, sugar, and saturated fats. Physical activity recommendations suggest 150 minutes moderate exercise weekly. Weight management maintains healthy BMI. Smoking cessation dramatically reduces health risks. Moderate alcohol consumption limits intake. Stress management techniques include meditation, yoga, and counseling. Sleep hygiene promotes 7-9 hours quality sleep nightly. Sun protection prevents skin damage. Safe sex practices prevent STIs. Injury prevention includes seatbelts, helmets, and fall prevention. Dental care maintains oral health. Mental health awareness reduces stigma and encourages help-seeking.
