# World Religions and Comparative Mythology

World religions are the major systems of belief, practice, and community that have shaped human civilisation for thousands of years. Comparative mythology studies the stories different cultures tell about creation, heroes, gods, and the meaning of existence — and the striking patterns that recur across traditions separated by thousands of miles and millennia. Understanding both is essential for grasping why people believe what they believe, how cultures differ, and what unites the human experience.

---

## Major World Religions

### Overview

| Religion | Origin | Followers | Core Text | Key Concept |
|----------|--------|-----------|-----------|-------------|
| **Hinduism** | India (~2000 BCE) | ~1.2 billion | Vedas; Upanishads; Bhagavad Gita | Dharma; karma; moksha; cycle of rebirth |
| **Judaism** | Middle East (~1800 BCE) | ~15 million | Torah; Talmuk; Tanakh | Monotheism; covenant; law (halakha) |
| **Buddhism** | India (~500 BCE) | ~500 million | Tripitaka; Sutras | Four Noble Truths; Eightfold Path; nirvana |
| **Jainism** | India (~600 BCE) | ~4 million | Agamas | Ahimsa (non-violence); multiple viewpoints |
| **Christianity** | Middle East (~30 CE) | ~2.4 billion | Bible (Old + New Testament) | Jesus as saviour; resurrection; grace |
| **Islam** | Arabia (~610 CE) | ~1.9 billion | Quran; Hadith | Tawhid (oneness of God); Five Pillars |
| **Sikhism** | India (~1500 CE) | ~30 million | Guru Granth Sahib | One God; equality; service |
| **Confucianism** | China (~500 BCE) | ~6 million (as religion) | Analects; Five Classics | Ren (benevolence); li (ritual); social harmony |
| **Daoism (Taoism)** | China (~400 BCE) | ~12 million | Dao De Jing; Zhuangzi | Dao (the Way); wu wei (non-action); naturalness |
| **Shinto** | Japan (ancient) | ~100 million | Kojiki; Nihon Shoki | Kami (spirits in nature); purity; ritual |

## Abrahamic Religions

### Shared Elements

| Element | Judaism | Christianity | Islam |
|---------|---------|-------------|-------|
| **God** | Yahweh (one God) | Trinity (Father, Son, Holy Spirit) | Allah (one God) |
| **Prophet** | Moses (primary); Abraham | Jesus (son of God / messiah) | Muhammad (final prophet) |
| **Scripture** | Torah; Tanakh | Bible (Old + New Testament) | Quran |
| **Afterlife** | Olam Ha-Ba (world to come); varied beliefs | Heaven; Hell; resurrection | Jannah (paradise); Jahannam (hell) |
| **Worship** | Synagogue; Shabbat; prayer | Church; Sunday; sacraments | Mosque; Friday prayer; Five Pillars |
| **Ethics** | Ten Commandments; justice; mercy | Sermon on the Mount; love; grace | Five Pillars; Sharia; justice; mercy |

### The Five Pillars of Islam

| Pillar | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Shahada** (Declaration of Faith) | "There is no god but God, and Muhammad is the messenger of God" |
| **Salat** (Prayer) | Five daily prayers facing Mecca |
| **Zakat** (Almsgiving) | Giving 2.5% of wealth to those in need |
| **Sawm** (Fasting) | Fasting during Ramadan from dawn to sunset |
| **Hajj** (Pilgrimage) | Pilgrimage to Mecca at least once in a lifetime (if able) |

## Eastern Religions

### Key Concepts

| Concept | Tradition | Description |
|---------|-----------|-------------|
| **Dharma** | Hinduism; Buddhism; Jainism | Duty; cosmic law; righteous conduct |
| **Karma** | Hinduism; Buddhism; Jainism | Actions have consequences across lifetimes |
| **Samsara** | Hinduism; Buddhism; Jainism | Cycle of birth, death, and rebirth |
| **Moksha** | Hinduism | Liberation from samsara; union with Brahman |
| **Nirvana** | Buddhism | Cessation of suffering; end of the cycle |
| **Ahimsa** | Jainism; Hinduism; Buddhism | Non-violence toward all living beings |
| **Dao** | Daoism | The fundamental principle underlying the universe |
| **Wu wei** | Daoism | Effortless action; acting in harmony with nature |
| **Kami** | Shinto | Spirits or essences present in all things |

### Buddhism: The Four Noble Truths

| Truth | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Dukkha** (Suffering) | Life involves suffering; dissatisfaction; impermanence |
| **Samudaya** (Origin) | Suffering arises from craving; attachment; ignorance |
| **Nirodha** (Cessation) | Suffering can end; by eliminating craving |
| **Magga** (Path) | The Eightfold Path leads to the cessation of suffering |

### The Eightfold Path

| Category | Path Factor | Description |
|----------|-----------|-------------|
| **Wisdom** | Right View | Understanding the Four Noble Truths |
| **Wisdom** | Right Intention | Renunciation; goodwill; harmlessness |
| **Ethics** | Right Speech | No lying; no harsh words; no gossip |
| **Ethics** | Right Action | No killing; no stealing; no sexual misconduct |
| **Ethics** | Right Livelihood | Earning a living without harming others |
| **Meditation** | Right Effort | Cultivating wholesome states; abandoning unwholesome |
| **Meditation** | Right Mindfulness | Awareness of body; feelings; mind; phenomena |
| **Meditation** | Right Concentration | Deep meditative absorption (jhana) |

## Comparative Mythology

### The Hero's Journey (Monomyth)

Joseph Campbell identified a pattern that recurs in myths across cultures:

| Stage | Description | Example |
|-------|-------------|---------|
| **Ordinary world** | Hero's normal life | Frodo in the Shire |
| **Call to adventure** | A challenge or quest appears | Gandalf arrives with the Ring |
| **Refusal of the call** | Hero hesitates | Frodo: "Why me?" |
| **Supernatural aid** | Mentor provides tools or guidance | Gandalf gives counsel; Elrond provides the fellowship |
| **Crossing the threshold** | Hero enters the unknown | Leaving the Shire |
| **Belly of the whale** | Complete separation from the known world | Mines of Moria |
| **Tests, allies, enemies** | Challenges; friendships; opposition | Various trials; forming bonds |
| **Approach to the inmost cave** | Preparing for the major challenge | Approaching Mount Doom |
| **Ordeal** | The central crisis; death and rebirth | Confrontation with the enemy |
| **Reward** | Gaining the treasure or knowledge | The Ring is destroyed |
| **The road back** | Return journey; final challenges | Return home |
| **Resurrection** | Final transformation; hero is changed | Frodo returns, but is forever changed |
| **Return with the elixir** | Hero brings wisdom or gift back to the ordinary world | The Shire is saved |

### Creation Myths

| Type | Description | Examples |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Ex nihilo** (from nothing) | God creates from nothing | Genesis (Abrahamic) |
| **Chaos** | Order emerges from primordial chaos | Greek (Chaos → Gaia); Norse (Ginnungagap) |
| **Cosmic egg** | Universe hatches from a primordial egg | Hindu (Brahmanda); Chinese (Pangu) |
| **World parent** | Sky father and earth mother separated | Maori (Rangi and Papa); Greek (Uranus and Gaia) |
| **Diving earth** | Animal dives into primordial water to create land | Native American; Japanese (Izanagi and Izanami) |
| **Emergence** | Beings ascend through worlds to the present one | Navajo; Hopi |

### Flood Myths

| Tradition | Story |
|-----------|-------|
| **Abrahamic** (Genesis) | God sends a flood; Noah builds an ark; saved with his family and animals |
| **Mesopotamian** (Epic of Gilgamesh) | Gods send a flood; Utnapishtim builds a boat; survives |
| **Hindu** | Vishnu (as Matsya, a fish) warns Manu of a flood; guides his boat to safety |
| **Greek** | Zeus sends a flood; Deucalion and Pyrrha survive by building an ark |
| **Mesoamerican** | Gods destroy humanity with a flood; a few survive |

## Religious Practices

### Common Practices Across Religions

| Practice | Description | Religions |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Prayer** | Communication with the divine | All major religions |
| **Meditation** | Contemplative practice; inner stillness | Buddhism; Hinduism; Christianity (contemplative); secular |
| **Fasting** | Abstaining from food for spiritual purposes | Islam (Ramadan); Christianity (Lent); Judaism (Yom Kippur) |
| **Pilgrimage** | Journey to a sacred place | Islam (Mecca); Christianity (Jerusalem); Hinduism (Varanasi); Buddhism (Bodh Gaya) |
| **Ritual sacrifice** | Offering to the divine | Historical in most traditions; symbolic in modern practice |
| **Scripture reading** | Study of sacred texts | All major religions |
| **Charity** | Giving to those in need | All major religions (zakat; tithing; dana) |
| **Rites of passage** | Marking life transitions | Birth; coming of age; marriage; death |

## Summary

World religions are humanity's oldest and most widespread attempts to answer fundamental questions: Why are we here? How should we live? What happens after death? The Abrahamic faiths (Judaism, Christianity, Islam) share a common ancestor in Abraham and emphasise monotheism, scripture, and ethical law. Eastern traditions (Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, Daoism) focus on dharma, karma, liberation from the cycle of rebirth, and harmony with nature. Comparative mythology reveals striking patterns across cultures — the hero's journey, creation myths, flood stories — suggesting deep structures in human storytelling. Religious practices (prayer, meditation, fasting, pilgrimage, charity) appear in every tradition. Secular worldviews (humanism, existentialism, stoicism) offer non-religious frameworks for meaning. Understanding these traditions is not about believing any of them — it's about understanding the billions of people whose lives, values, and decisions are shaped by them.
