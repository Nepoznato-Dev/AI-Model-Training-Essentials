# Geography and Geopolitics

Geography shapes destiny. The location of mountains, rivers, and natural resources has determined trade routes, sparked wars, and built civilisations for millennia. Today, geopolitics — the interplay of geography, power, and politics — remains essential for understanding why nations behave the way they do.

## Geopolitical Concepts

### Key Terms

| Concept | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| **Sovereignty** | Supreme authority within a territory |
| **EEZ** | Exclusive Economic Zone — 200 nautical miles from coast, resource rights |
| **Territorial Waters** | 12 nautical miles from baseline, full sovereignty |
| **Enclave** | Territory surrounded by another state (e.g., Lesotho, Vatican City) |
| **Buffer State** | Country between rival powers (historical: Afghanistan, Mongolia) |
| **Shatterbelt** | Region caught between stronger external powers |
| **Stateless Nation** | Ethnic group without its own state (Kurds, Palestinians, Tibetans) |

### Classic Geopolitical Theories

| Theorist | Theory | Core Idea |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| **Mackinder** (1861–1947) | Heartland Theory | "Who rules East Europe commands the Heartland" |
| **Mahan** (1840–1914) | Sea Power | Control of the seas = global dominance |
| **Spykman** (1893–1943) | Rimland Theory | Coastal regions more important than the interior |

### Power and Influence

| Concept | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Hard Power** | Military force, economic sanctions, coercion |
| **Soft Power** | Cultural influence, diplomacy, values attraction |
| **Smart Power** | Combination of hard and soft |
| **Monroe Doctrine** | US opposition to European colonisation in the Americas (1823) |
| **Belt and Road** | Chinese infrastructure investments creating economic influence |

## Regional Geopolitics

### Middle East and North Africa

The MENA region holds ~48% of the world's proven oil reserves and remains one of the most geopolitically complex areas on Earth. Key dynamics: oil wealth, water scarcity, religious divisions (Sunni-Shia), and the Arab-Israeli conflict. Major powers include Saudi Arabia, Iran, Turkey, Israel, Egypt, and the UAE.

### Asia-Pacific

East Asia is defined by the rise of China, Japan's economic strength, South Korea's tech leadership, and North Korea's nuclear programme. South Asia is dominated by India's demographic weight. Southeast Asia navigates between major powers through ASEAN cooperation. Key flashpoints: Taiwan Strait, South China Sea, India-China border, Korean Peninsula.

### Europe

European integration has been the defining project since WWII — but Brexit (2020), the Russia-Ukraine conflict (2022 invasion), and energy dependence have tested the EU's unity. NATO expansion remains a central geopolitical tension.

### Africa

Africa's challenges are rooted in colonial legacy — artificial borders, ethnic divisions, and language policies — but the continent is also home to rising powers: Nigeria (largest economy), South Africa (most industrialised), and Ethiopia (one of the fastest-growing economies).

### Americas

North America centres on US-Canada-Mexico relations (USMCA), immigration, and trade. Latin America grapples with inequality, drug trafficking, and populist movements. The Caribbean remains tourism-dependent and climate-vulnerable.

## Contemporary Issues

### Territorial Disputes

| Dispute | Parties | Core Issue |
|---------|---------|-----------|
| **South China Sea** | China vs Philippines, Vietnam, Malaysia, Taiwan | Nine-dash line claims |
| **Kashmir** | India, Pakistan, China | Competing territorial claims |
| **Crimea** | Russia / Ukraine | Annexed 2014; internationally recognised as Ukrainian |
| **Taiwan** | China / Taiwan | China claims sovereignty; Taiwan operates independently |
| **Western Sahara** | Morocco / Polisario Front | Self-determination dispute |

### Climate and Geography

Climate change is reshaping geopolitics: rising sea levels threaten island nations (Maldives, Tuvalu) and coastal cities; Arctic melting opens new shipping routes and resource claims; climate migration is accelerating; and water conflicts intensify (Nile Dam dispute between Ethiopia, Egypt, and Sudan).

### Resource Geopolitics

The energy transition is creating new dependencies. Critical minerals — lithium, cobalt, rare earths — are the new "oil." China controls ~80% of rare earth processing. Taiwan produces ~60% of the world's semiconductor chips, making it a geopolitical flashpoint far beyond its size.

## GIS and Mapping

Geographic Information Systems (GIS) have revolutionised how we understand and interact with spatial data.

| Application | Use |
|-------------|-----|
| **Urban Planning** | Land use, transportation, zoning |
| **Environmental Monitoring** | Deforestation, pollution, climate change tracking |
| **Disaster Response** | Emergency management, evacuation routes |
| **Business Intelligence** | Market analysis, site selection, logistics |
| **Public Health** | Disease tracking, healthcare access mapping |

Core technologies include GPS, remote sensing (satellite imagery), spatial analysis, and modern cartography.

## Why Geography Still Matters

In an age of digital connectivity, it's tempting to think geography doesn't matter anymore. It does. Mountains still define borders. Rivers still supply water to billions. Oil still fuels economies. Chokepoints like the Strait of Hormuz, Malacca, and Suez still control global trade. And the physical landscape continues to shape who has power, who lacks it, and how nations interact.
