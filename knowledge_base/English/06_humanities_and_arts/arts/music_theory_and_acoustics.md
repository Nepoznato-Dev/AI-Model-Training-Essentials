# Music Theory and Acoustics

Music theory is the study of how music works — the patterns, structures, and principles that make some combinations of sounds pleasing or meaningful and others discordant or chaotic. Acoustics is the physics of sound — how vibrations travel through air, how they're produced and perceived. Together, they explain why a C major chord sounds happy, why a minor key feels sad, how concert halls are designed, and why your favourite song gives you chills.

---

## The Physics of Sound

### Sound Fundamentals

| Property | Description | Perception |
|----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Frequency** | Number of vibrations per second (Hz) | Pitch (higher frequency = higher pitch) |
| **Amplitude** | Size of the vibration | Loudness (greater amplitude = louder) |
| **Waveform** | Shape of the vibration | Timbre (why a piano and guitar sound different playing the same note) |
| **Duration** | How long the vibration lasts | Note length; rhythm |

### The Harmonic Series

| Partial | Frequency (relative to fundamental) | Interval | Perceived Quality |
|---------|-------------------------------------|----------|-------------------|
| **1st (fundamental)** | f | Unison | The pitch you hear |
| **2nd** | 2f | Octave | Very consonant |
| **3rd** | 3f | Octave + fifth | Consonant |
| **4th** | 4f | Two octaves | Very consonant |
| **5th** | 5f | Two octaves + major third | Moderately consonant |
| **6th** | 6f | Two octaves + fifth | Consonant |
| **7th** | 7f | Two octaves + minor seventh | Dissonant |

The harmonic series explains why certain intervals sound consonant — their harmonics overlap and align.

## Pitch and Scales

### Musical Intervals

| Interval | Semitones | Ratio | Character | Example |
|----------|-----------|-------|-----------|---------|
| **Unison** | 0 | 1:1 | Same pitch | C to C |
| **Minor 2nd** | 1 | 16:15 | Tense; dissonant | C to Db |
| **Major 2nd** | 2 | 9:8 | Step; mild tension | C to D |
| **Minor 3rd** | 3 | 6:5 | Sad; dark | C to Eb |
| **Major 3rd** | 4 | 5:4 | Happy; bright | C to E |
| **Perfect 4th** | 5 | 4:3 | Open; ambiguous | C to F |
| **Tritone** | 6 | √2:1 | Very dissonant; unstable | C to F# |
| **Perfect 5th** | 7 | 3:2 | Strong; consonant | C to G |
| **Minor 6th** | 8 | 8:5 | Dark; rich | C to Ab |
| **Major 6th** | 9 | 5:3 | Sweet; consonant | C to A |
| **Minor 7th** | 10 | 9:5 | Bluesy; relaxed | C to Bb |
| **Major 7th** | 11 | 15:8 | Dreamy; tense | C to B |
| **Octave** | 12 | 2:1 | Same note class | C to C |

### Common Scales

| Scale | Pattern (W = whole step, H = half step) | Character | Example (from C) |
|-------|----------------------------------------|-----------|-------------------|
| **Major (Ionian)** | W-W-H-W-W-W-H | Happy; bright | C D E F G A B C |
| **Natural Minor (Aeolian)** | W-H-W-W-H-W-W | Sad; dark | C D Eb F G Ab Bb C |
| **Harmonic Minor** | W-H-W-W-H-W+H-H | Exotic; dramatic | C D Eb F G Ab B C |
| **Melodic Minor** (ascending) | W-H-W-W-W-W-H | Jazz; sophisticated | C D Eb F G A B C |
| **Dorian** | W-H-W-W-W-H-W | Minor but bright | C D Eb F G A Bb C |
| **Mixolydian** | W-W-H-W-W-H-W | Bluesy; rock | C D E F G A Bb C |
| **Pentatonic (major)** | W-W-min3-W-min3 | Open; folk; blues | C D E G A C |
| **Blues** | min3-W-H-H-min3-W | Bluesy; soulful | C Eb F F# G Bb C |
| **Chromatic** | All half steps | Tense; atonal | C C# D D# E F F# G G# A A# B C |

## Harmony and Chords

### Triads

| Chord | Intervals | Character | Example (from C) |
|-------|-----------|-----------|-------------------|
| **Major** | Root + M3 + P5 | Happy; stable | C-E-G |
| **Minor** | Root + m3 + P5 | Sad; stable | C-Eb-G |
| **Diminished** | Root + m3 + d5 | Tense; unstable | C-Eb-Gb |
| **Augmented** | Root + M3 + A5 | Mysterious; unstable | C-E-G# |

### Seventh Chords

| Chord | Intervals | Character | Typical Use |
|-------|-----------|-----------|-------------|
| **Major 7th** | Major triad + M7 | Dreamy; lush | Jazz; lo-fi |
| **Dominant 7th** | Major triad + m7 | Bluesy; wants to resolve | V chord in major key |
| **Minor 7th** | Minor triad + m7 | Smooth; relaxed | Jazz; R&B; funk |
| **Half-diminished 7th** | Diminished triad + m7 | Tense; ambiguous | ii chord in minor; jazz |
| **Diminished 7th** | Diminished triad + d7 | Very tense; dramatic | Passing chord; modulation |

### Chord Progressions

| Progression | Name | Character | Example Songs |
|-------------|------|-----------|---------------|
| **I-IV-V-I** | Standard cadence | Resolved; classical | "Twist and Shout"; "La Bamba" |
| **I-V-vi-IV** | Pop progression | Uplifting; familiar | Thousands of pop songs |
| **ii-V-I** | Jazz cadence | Smooth; sophisticated | Jazz standards |
| **I-vi-IV-V** | 50s progression | Nostalgic; doo-wop | "Stand By Me"; "Earth Angel" |
| **vi-IV-I-V** | Minor pop | Emotional; anthemic | Many modern pop songs |
| **I-IV-vi-V** | Variant | Slightly different emotional arc | "With or Without You" |
| **12-bar blues** | I-I-I-I-IV-IV-I-I-V-IV-I-V | Blues standard | Countless blues and rock songs |

## Rhythm and Meter

### Time Signatures

| Signature | Beats per Bar | Note Value | Feel | Example |
|-----------|--------------|------------|------|---------|
| **4/4** (common time) | 4 | Quarter note | Standard; rock; pop | Most popular music |
| **3/4** (waltz time) | 3 | Quarter note | Swinging; dance | "The Blue Danube" |
| **6/8** | 6 | Eighth note | Compound; lilting | "House of the Rising Sun" |
| **2/4** | 2 | Quarter note | March-like | "The Stars and Stripes Forever" |
| **5/4** | 5 | Quarter note | Odd; unsettling | "Take Five" (Dave Brubeck) |
| **7/8** | 7 | Eighth note | Odd; complex | "Money" (Pink Floyd) |

### Rhythmic Concepts

| Concept | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Tempo** | Speed of the beat (measured in BPM — beats per minute) |
| **Syncopation** | Accenting off-beats; creating rhythmic tension |
| **Swing** | Unequal subdivision of the beat (long-short pattern) |
| **Polyrhythm** | Two or more conflicting rhythms simultaneously (e.g., 3 against 4) |
| **Groove** | The felt sense of rhythmic momentum; "pocket" |
| **Rubato** | Expressive speeding up and slowing down of tempo |

## Musical Forms

| Form | Description | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Binary (AB)** | Two contrasting sections | Baroque dance suites |
| **Ternary (ABA)** | Statement; contrast; return | Minuet and trio; "Twinkle Twinkle" variations |
| **Sonata form** | Exposition (themes); development (exploration); recapitulation (return) | Classical symphonies; Beethoven |
| **Rondo (ABACA)** | Main theme alternates with episodes | Classical finales |
| **Verse-chorus** | Alternating verse (new lyrics) and chorus (repeated) | Pop; rock; country |
| **12-bar blues** | Three 4-bar phrases (AAB lyric structure) | Blues; early rock |
| **Through-composed** | No repeated sections; continuous new material | Art songs; progressive rock |

## Acoustics

### Room Acoustics

| Property | Description | Important For |
|----------|-------------|---------------|
| **Reverberation time (RT60)** | Time for sound to decay by 60 dB | Concert halls (1.5-2.5s); recording studios (0.3-0.5s) |
| **Early reflections** | Sound arriving within 50ms of direct sound | Clarity; intimacy |
| **Standing waves** | Resonant frequencies of a room | Bass buildup; nulls at certain positions |
| **Diffusion** | Scattering of sound in multiple directions | Even sound distribution |
| **Absorption** | Materials that reduce sound energy | Reducing echo; controlling reverberation |
| **Flutter echo** | Rapid repeating reflections between parallel surfaces | Undesirable; fixed with diffusion or absorption |

### Musical Instrument Families

| Family | Sound Production | Examples |
|--------|-----------------|---------|
| **Strings** | Vibrating strings (bowed, plucked, struck) | Violin; guitar; piano; harp |
| **Woodwind** | Vibrating air column; reed or edge | Flute; clarinet; saxophone; oboe |
| **Brass** | Vibrating lips in a cup mouthpiece | Trumpet; trombone; French horn; tuba |
| **Percussion (pitched)** | Struck; produces definite pitch | Timpani; marimba; xylophone |
| **Percussion (unpitched)** | Struck; produces indefinite pitch | Snare drum; cymbals; bass drum |
| **Electronic** | Oscillators; digital synthesis | Synthesiser; drum machine; theremin |

## Summary

Music theory explains the patterns that make music work. Sound is vibration — characterised by frequency (pitch), amplitude (loudness), and waveform (timbre). The harmonic series explains why certain intervals sound consonant. Scales are ordered sequences of notes that define a musical system — major scales sound bright; minor scales sound dark; modes offer other colours. Chords are built from stacked intervals — major triads are stable; diminished triads are tense; seventh chords add colour and complexity. Chord progressions create expectation and resolution. Rhythm organises time through meter, tempo, syncopation, and groove. Musical forms provide structure — from binary and ternary to sonata form to verse-chorus. Acoustics explains how sound behaves in spaces — reverberation, reflection, absorption, and diffusion determine whether a room sounds good. Understanding these fundamentals enriches both the creation and appreciation of music.
