# English Dictionary — Common Word Definitions

A reference list of frequently used English words and their meanings,
covering everyday vocabulary, academic terms, and words commonly encountered
in questions and discussions.

---

## A

**abandon** — to leave behind permanently; to give up on a goal or place.
**ability** — the power or skill to do something.
**abstract** — existing as a concept rather than a concrete thing; a summary of a document.
**accurate** — correct and precise; free from errors.
**achieve** — to successfully reach a goal through effort.
**acknowledge** — to accept or admit the truth of something; to show recognition.
**acquire** — to obtain or gain something, usually through effort or purchase.
**adapt** — to adjust to new conditions or requirements.
**adequate** — satisfactory; just enough for a purpose.
**adjacent** — next to or adjoining something else.
**adjust** — to make small changes to improve fit or function.
**advance** — to move forward; progress; also, ahead of the current time.
**advantage** — a condition that gives a favourable position over others.
**adverse** — unfavourable; harmful.
**affect** — to have an impact on something (verb); emotion (noun, formal).
**aggregate** — a whole formed by combining several elements.
**algorithm** — a set of rules or steps for solving a problem or task.
**allocate** — to assign resources for a particular purpose.
**alter** — to change something, usually partially.
**ambiguous** — unclear; can be interpreted in more than one way.
**analyse** — to examine in detail to understand or explain.
**annotate** — to add explanatory notes to a text or diagram.
**anticipate** — to expect or prepare for something before it happens.
**apparent** — clearly visible or understood; seeming to be true.
**approach** — a way of dealing with something; to come near.
**approximate** — nearly correct; not exact.
**assess** — to evaluate or judge the quality, value, or nature of something.
**assign** — to give a task or role to someone; to allocate.
**assume** — to accept something as true without proof.
**attribute** — a quality or feature; to credit something to a cause (verb).
**automate** — to make a process operate automatically without human intervention.

## B

**benefit** — an advantage or positive outcome.
**bias** — an unfair preference for or against something; systematic error.
**brief** — short in time or length; a summary or instructions.

## C

**calculate** — to determine a value using mathematics.
**capable** — having the ability or qualities needed for something.
**capacity** — the maximum amount something can hold or do.
**categorise** — to place into groups based on shared characteristics.
**clarify** — to make something easier to understand.
**classify** — to arrange into categories or groups.
**coherent** — logical and consistent; easy to understand.
**collaborate** — to work together with others towards a common goal.
**compile** — to gather and organise information; to convert source code into executable code.
**complex** — consisting of many interconnected parts; not simple.
**component** — a part or element of a larger whole.
**comprehend** — to understand fully.
**compute** — to calculate or process data.
**concept** — an abstract idea or general notion.
**conclude** — to reach a decision or finish something.
**condition** — a requirement; a state of being; circumstances.
**configure** — to arrange or set up a system in a particular way.
**confirm** — to establish the truth or accuracy of something.
**conflict** — a serious disagreement; a clash between opposing forces.
**consistent** — always behaving in the same way; not contradictory.
**context** — the circumstances surrounding something; background information.
**contrast** — to compare in order to show differences.
**convert** — to change from one form, unit, or use to another.
**coordinate** — to organise different elements to work together.
**criteria** — standards by which something is judged (plural of criterion).

## D

**data** — facts and statistics collected for analysis.
**debug** — to find and fix errors in computer code.
**define** — to give the meaning of a word or describe the limits of something.
**demonstrate** — to show how something works or is done.
**deploy** — to put into use; to install and run software in a live environment.
**derive** — to obtain something from a source; to reach a conclusion by reasoning.
**determine** — to find out or establish something conclusively.
**develop** — to build, grow, or make something more elaborate over time.
**differ** — to be unlike; to disagree.
**distinct** — clearly different and separate.
**distribute** — to spread or share across multiple locations or people.
**domain** — a field of knowledge or activity; a web address.
**duplicate** — an exact copy; to copy something.

## E

**efficient** — achieving results with minimum waste of time or resources.
**element** — a basic component; one of the chemical substances in the periodic table.
**embed** — to fix firmly within; to incorporate into a larger structure.
**enable** — to make possible; to activate a feature.
**enhance** — to improve the quality or value of something.
**entity** — a thing with distinct existence; an object in a system.
**enumerate** — to list items one by one; to count.
**environment** — surroundings; in computing, the system and configuration in which software runs.
**error** — a mistake; a deviation from correct behaviour.
**estimate** — an approximate calculation; to judge roughly.
**evaluate** — to form an opinion after careful consideration.
**execute** — to carry out a plan or instruction; to run a computer program.
**explicit** — clearly stated and leaving nothing implied.
**extend** — to make longer or larger; to add functionality.
**extract** — to take out or obtain from a source.

## F

**factor** — an element that contributes to a result; to break into components (math).
**feature** — a distinctive aspect or capability.
**filter** — to remove unwanted elements; a device or function that selects items.
**flexible** — able to adapt to different conditions.
**format** — the arrangement or layout of data; to prepare for use.
**framework** — a basic structure providing support for building something.
**function** — the purpose of something; a reusable block of code (programming).
**fundamental** — forming the base or foundation; essential.

## G

**generate** — to produce or create.
**global** — relating to the whole world; applying to an entire system.
**guideline** — a general rule or recommendation.

## H

**hierarchy** — a system ranked by levels of authority or importance.
**hypothesis** — a proposed explanation that can be tested.

## I

**identify** — to recognise and name something.
**implement** — to put a plan or system into effect.
**implicit** — implied without being directly stated.
**index** — a pointer to data location; an alphabetical list (noun); to categorise (verb).
**infer** — to reach a conclusion from evidence without explicit statement.
**initialise** — to set a variable or system to a starting state.
**input** — data provided to a system; to enter data.
**integrate** — to combine parts into a whole; to add a feature to an existing system.
**interface** — a point where two systems meet and interact; a contract in programming.
**interpret** — to explain the meaning of something; to execute code line by line.
**iterate** — to repeat a process, often with incremental changes.

## J

**justify** — to give reasons for a decision; to align text along a margin.

## K

**key** — essential; a value used to look up data; a cryptographic secret.

## L

**layer** — a level in a hierarchy; a component stacked on others.
**legacy** — inherited from the past; old software still in use.
**limit** — a boundary; the maximum or minimum value allowed.
**linear** — arranged in a straight line; proportional increase.
**link** — a connection between two things; a hyperlink.
**local** — relating to a nearby area; in computing, running on the same machine.
**logic** — reasoning according to rules; the flow of control in a program.

## M

**maintain** — to keep in good condition; to continue over time.
**manipulate** — to handle or control, sometimes with skill; to influence unfairly.
**map** — to associate keys with values (data structure); to show spatial relationships.
**merge** — to combine into one; to join code branches.
**method** — a way of doing something; a function attached to an object (OOP).
**migrate** — to move data or systems from one environment to another.
**model** — a representation of something; a trained AI system.
**modify** — to change partially.
**monitor** — to observe and check over time; a display screen.

## N

**navigate** — to find a path through; to move between pages or screens.
**node** — a point in a network or tree structure.
**normalise** — to scale values to a standard range; to reduce redundancy in a database.

## O

**objective** — a goal or aim; not influenced by personal feelings (adjective).
**obtain** — to get or acquire.
**optimise** — to make as effective or efficient as possible.
**output** — data produced by a system; the result of a computation.
**override** — to take control over an existing setting or method.

## P

**parameter** — a value passed to a function; a measurable characteristic.
**parse** — to analyse text into components; to interpret structured data.
**pattern** — a repeated arrangement; a template for solving a common problem.
**persist** — to continue to exist; to save data so it survives restarts.
**pipeline** — a series of connected stages where the output of one feeds the next.
**precise** — exact; accurate to fine detail.
**predict** — to say what will happen before it does.
**prioritise** — to rank tasks by importance and address the most important first.
**process** — a series of steps; a running program instance (verb: to handle or transform).
**prompt** — a cue or instruction given to an AI model or user.
**property** — a characteristic or attribute; a value belonging to an object.
**protocol** — a set of rules for communication between systems.

## Q

**query** — a request for information from a database; a question.
**queue** — a line of items waiting to be processed (first in, first out).

## R

**recursive** — a function or process that calls or refers to itself.
**redundant** — unnecessary because it duplicates existing functionality; extra for backup.
**refactor** — to restructure existing code without changing its external behaviour.
**reference** — a pointer to a value; a source cited for information.
**relevant** — closely connected to the topic at hand.
**render** — to produce output (graphics, text, or HTML).
**represent** — to stand for or describe something.
**requirement** — a necessary condition or feature.
**resolve** — to find a solution; to determine a value (e.g. a DNS name).
**retrieve** — to get back stored information.
**robust** — strong and reliable, especially under stress.

## S

**scalable** — able to handle increased workload without degrading performance.
**schema** — the structure or layout of a database or data format.
**scope** — the range of effect; the section of code where a variable is visible.
**segment** — a distinct section or part.
**semantic** — relating to meaning, especially in language or code.
**sequence** — a set of things in a particular order.
**session** — a period of activity; a connection between client and server.
**simulate** — to imitate the behaviour of a system.
**source** — the origin of something; original code before compilation.
**specify** — to state clearly and in detail.
**static** — not changing; in computing, fixed at compile time or not associated with instances.
**store** — to save data for later use.
**structure** — the arrangement of parts; a composite data type.
**summarise** — to give a brief statement of the main points.
**synchronise** — to cause to happen at the same time or rate.
**syntax** — the rules governing the structure of statements in a language.

## T

**template** — a model or pattern used as a starting point.
**threshold** — a level or point that triggers an action when reached.
**token** — a unit of text in NLP; an authentication credential.
**trace** — to follow a path; a record of program execution for debugging.
**transform** — to change in form, nature, or appearance.
**transmit** — to send data from one place to another.

## U

**uniform** — consistent and unchanged throughout.
**unique** — the only one of its kind; occurring just once.
**update** — to bring to a more current state.
**utilise** — to make use of something.

## V

**validate** — to confirm something meets requirements or is correct.
**variable** — a named container for a value that can change.
**verify** — to check correctness or truth.
**version** — a specific form of a product at a particular point in its development.

## W

**workflow** — a series of steps followed to complete a task.
