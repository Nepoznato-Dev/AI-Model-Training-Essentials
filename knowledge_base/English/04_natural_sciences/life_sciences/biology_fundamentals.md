# Biology Fundamentals

Biology is the science of life. All living things share certain features: they are made of cells, they use energy, they grow, they respond to their environment, they reproduce, and they evolve. Biology spans from the molecular machinery inside a single cell to the interactions of entire ecosystems, and it is the foundation of medicine, agriculture, biotechnology, and conservation.

---

## Cells — The Basic Unit

Every living thing is made of cells. There are two fundamental types:

### Prokaryotic vs Eukaryotic

| Feature | Prokaryotic | Eukaryotic |
|---------|-------------|------------|
| **Nucleus** | No — DNA floats freely | Yes — DNA enclosed in membrane |
| **Size** | Small (1-5 μm) | Larger (10-100 μm) |
| **Examples** | Bacteria, archaea | Animals, plants, fungi, protists |
| **Complexity** | Simple | Complex internal structures (organelles) |
| **Reproduction** | Binary fission | Mitosis or meiosis |
| **DNA form** | Single circular chromosome | Multiple linear chromosomes |
| **Organelles** | None (ribosomes only) | Mitochondria, ER, Golgi, lysosomes, etc. |

### Key Organelles

| Organelle | Function | Found In |
|-----------|----------|----------|
| **Nucleus** | Stores DNA; controls cell activities | Eukaryotes only |
| **Mitochondria** | Cellular respiration; produces ATP (energy currency) | Eukaryotes only |
| **Chloroplasts** | Photosynthesis; converts light energy to chemical energy | Plant cells only |
| **Endoplasmic reticulum** | Protein and lipid synthesis | Eukaryotes only |
| **Golgi apparatus** | Modifies, packages, and ships proteins | Eukaryotes only |
| **Ribosomes** | Protein synthesis (translate mRNA) | All cells |
| **Cell membrane** | Controls what enters/exits the cell | All cells |
| **Cell wall** | Structural support and protection | Plants, fungi, bacteria |
| **Vacuole** | Storage; maintains turgor pressure in plants | All (large in plants) |
| **Lysosomes** | Digestion and waste removal | Animal cells primarily |

## DNA and Genetics

DNA is the instruction manual for building and running a living organism. It is a double helix made of four bases: adenine (A), thymine (T), cytosine (C), and guanine (G). A always pairs with T; C always pairs with G. This pairing is how DNA copies itself.

### From DNA to Protein

| Step | Process | Where | What Happens |
|------|---------|-------|-------------|
| **Replication** | DNA → DNA | Nucleus | DNA unwinds; each strand serves as template for a new copy |
| **Transcription** | DNA → mRNA | Nucleus | A gene is copied into messenger RNA |
| **Translation** | mRNA → Protein | Ribosome | mRNA is read in triplets (codons); each codon specifies an amino acid |

This flow — DNA → RNA → Protein — is called the **central dogma** of molecular biology.

### Genes and Chromosomes

Genes are sections of DNA that code for proteins. The human genome contains roughly 20,000 genes packed into 23 pairs of chromosomes. Mutations — changes in the DNA sequence — are the raw material of evolution.

| Mutation Type | Description | Example Effect |
|---------------|-------------|----------------|
| **Point mutation** | Single base change (A → G) | Sickle cell anemia (one base change in hemoglobin gene) |
| **Insertion** | Extra bases added | Frameshift; often disruptive |
| **Deletion** | Bases removed | Frameshift; can cause disease |
| **Duplication** | Segment copied | Extra gene copy; can lead to new functions over time |
| **Chromosomal** | Large-scale rearrangement | Translocations; can cause cancer or infertility |

Most mutations are harmless, some are beneficial, and a few cause disease.

## Evolution by Natural Selection

Evolution is not "just a theory" in the colloquial sense — it is the unifying framework of all biology. The mechanism is straightforward:

1. Organisms produce more offspring than the environment can support.
2. Individuals vary in their traits (some traits are heritable).
3. Individuals with traits better suited to the environment are more likely to survive and reproduce.
4. Over generations, advantageous traits become more common in the population.

This process — without any guiding intelligence — explains the diversity of all life on Earth, from bacteria to blue whales.

### Evidence for Evolution

| Evidence Type | Description |
|---------------|-------------|
| **Fossil record** | Shows gradual change over billions of years; transitional forms (Archaeopteryx, Tiktaalik) |
| **Comparative anatomy** | Homologous structures (human arm, whale flipper, bat wing share same bone layout) |
| **Molecular biology** | DNA and protein sequences are more similar in closely related species |
| **Biogeography** | Species distribution matches continental drift and isolation patterns |
| **Observed evolution** | Antibiotic resistance in bacteria; pesticide resistance in insects; lab experiments |

### Mechanisms of Evolution

| Mechanism | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Natural selection** | Advantageous traits become more common over generations |
| **Genetic drift** | Random changes in allele frequencies; stronger in small populations |
| **Gene flow** | Migration of individuals (and their genes) between populations |
| **Mutation** | New genetic variation introduced by DNA changes |
| **Sexual selection** | Traits that improve mating success become more common (peacock tail) |
| **Speciation** | Populations diverge until they can no longer interbreed |

## Ecology

Ecology studies how organisms interact with each other and with their environment.

### Levels of Organization

| Level | Description | Example |
|-------|-------------|---------|
| **Organism** | Individual living thing | One deer |
| **Population** | Group of same species in an area | Herd of deer |
| **Community** | All populations in an area | Deer + rabbits + birds + insects + plants |
| **Ecosystem** | Community + physical environment | Forest + all organisms + soil + water + climate |
| **Biosphere** | All ecosystems on Earth | Everything from deep ocean to upper atmosphere |

### Energy Flow

| Concept | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Producers** | Convert sunlight to energy (plants, algae, cyanobacteria) |
| **Primary consumers** | Eat producers (herbivores: deer, rabbits) |
| **Secondary consumers** | Eat primary consumers (carnivores: wolves) |
| **Tertiary consumers** | Top predators (eagles, orcas) |
| **Decomposers** | Break down dead matter (fungi, bacteria) |

Only about 10% of energy transfers between trophic levels — the rest is lost as heat. This is why food chains rarely exceed 4-5 levels and why there are fewer predators than prey.

### Biogeochemical Cycles

| Cycle | Key Process |
|-------|-------------|
| **Carbon cycle** | Photosynthesis removes CO₂; respiration and combustion release it |
| **Nitrogen cycle** | Nitrogen-fixing bacteria convert N₂ to usable forms; denitrifying bacteria return it |
| **Water cycle** | Evaporation → condensation → precipitation → runoff → repeat |
| **Phosphorus cycle** | Weathering of rocks releases phosphorus; no atmospheric phase |

## Taxonomy and the Tree of Life

All living things are classified into a hierarchy: Domain → Kingdom → Phylum → Class → Order → Family → Genus → Species.

### The Three Domains

| Domain | Cell Type | Key Features | Examples |
|--------|-----------|-------------|---------|
| **Bacteria** | Prokaryotic | Peptidoglycan cell walls; most familiar bacteria | E. coli, Streptococcus |
| **Archaea** | Prokaryotic | No peptidoglycan; often extremophiles | Methanogens, halophiles, thermophiles |
| **Eukarya** | Eukaryotic | Membrane-bound nucleus and organelles | Everything else |

### Kingdoms within Eukarya

| Kingdom | Key Features | Examples |
|---------|-------------|---------|
| **Animalia** | Multicellular; heterotrophic; no cell walls | Humans, insects, fish, birds |
| **Plantae** | Multicellular; autotrophic (photosynthesis); cellulose cell walls | Trees, grasses, ferns, mosses |
| **Fungi** | Mostly multicellular; heterotrophic (absorption); chitin cell walls | Mushrooms, yeast, mold |
| **Protista** | Mostly unicellular; diverse | Amoeba, algae, paramecium |

## Summary

Biology is the science of life, from molecules to ecosystems. All living things are made of cells — either simple prokaryotes (bacteria, archaea) or complex eukaryotes (animals, plants, fungi). DNA carries genetic information through the central dogma: DNA → RNA → protein. Evolution by natural selection is the unifying framework, supported by fossils, anatomy, molecular biology, biogeography, and direct observation. Ecology studies how organisms interact with each other and their environment, with energy flowing from producers through consumers and decomposers. The tree of life classifies all organisms into three domains — Bacteria, Archaea, and Eukarya — with further subdivision into kingdoms, phyla, and species. Biology is foundational to medicine, agriculture, biotechnology, and conservation.
