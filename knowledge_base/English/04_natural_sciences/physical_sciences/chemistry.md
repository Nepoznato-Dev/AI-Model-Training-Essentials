# Chemistry

If physics is the rules of the game, chemistry is what happens when the pieces interact. Chemistry studies how atoms bond, break apart, and rearrange — which is to say, how all materials behave. It bridges the gap between fundamental physics and the complexity of biology, geology, and engineering.

---

## Atomic Structure

### The Atom in 30 Seconds

Atoms have a nucleus (protons + neutrons) surrounded by electrons in shells. The number of protons defines the element (1 = hydrogen, 6 = carbon, 79 = gold). The number of neutrons determines the isotope (carbon-12 vs. carbon-14). The arrangement of electrons determines how the atom bonds with other atoms.

| Particle | Charge | Mass (amu) | Location |
|----------|--------|-----------|----------|
| **Proton** | +1 | ~1 | Nucleus |
| **Neutron** | 0 | ~1 | Nucleus |
| **Electron** | −1 | ~1/1836 | Electron shells |

### Electron Configuration

Electrons occupy shells (energy levels) around the nucleus. Each shell holds a maximum number of electrons:

| Shell | Max Electrons | Subshells | Distance from Nucleus |
|-------|--------------|-----------|----------------------|
| **1 (K)** | 2 | 1s | Closest |
| **2 (L)** | 8 | 2s, 2p | |
| **3 (M)** | 18 | 3s, 3p, 3d | |
| **4 (N)** | 32 | 4s, 4p, 4d, 4f | Farthest (valence) |

The outermost shell (valence shell) determines chemical behavior. Atoms with full valence shells (noble gases) are inert; atoms with incomplete shells react to achieve stability.

## The Periodic Table

The periodic table is not a random list — it is a map of chemical behavior. Elements in the same column (group) behave similarly because they have the same number of valence electrons.

### Key Groups

| Group | Elements | Behavior |
|-------|----------|----------|
| **Alkali metals** (Group 1) | Li, Na, K... | Extremely reactive, lose 1 electron, explode in water |
| **Alkaline earth metals** (Group 2) | Be, Mg, Ca... | Reactive, lose 2 electrons, form basic oxides |
| **Halogens** (Group 17) | F, Cl, Br, I... | Very reactive, gain 1 electron, form salts |
| **Noble gases** (Group 18) | He, Ne, Ar... | Almost completely inert — full electron shells |
| **Transition metals** (Groups 3-12) | Fe, Cu, Au... | Variable oxidation states, form colored compounds |
| **Lanthanides** (57-71) | Ce, Nd, Eu... | Rare earth elements; used in magnets, lasers, phosphors |
| **Actinides** (89-103) | U, Pu, Am... | All radioactive; uranium and plutonium used in nuclear energy |

### Periodic Trends

| Trend | Across a Period (→) | Down a Group (↓) |
|-------|---------------------|-------------------|
| **Atomic radius** | Decreases (more protons pull electrons tighter) | Increases (more electron shells) |
| **Ionization energy** | Increases (harder to remove electrons) | Decreases (outer electrons farther from nucleus) |
| **Electronegativity** | Increases (stronger pull on shared electrons) | Decreases |
| **Metallic character** | Decreases | Increases |

## Chemical Bonding

Atoms bond to achieve stable electron configurations. The three main types:

### Bond Types

| Bond Type | Mechanism | Strength | Example |
|-----------|-----------|----------|---------|
| **Ionic** | One atom gives up an electron, another accepts it. Opposite charges attract. | Strong | NaCl (table salt) |
| **Covalent** | Atoms share electrons. | Stronger than ionic | H₂O (water), CO₂, DNA |
| **Metallic** | Electrons flow freely between atoms, creating a "sea" of charge. | Variable | Fe, Cu, Au (all metals) |

### Intermolecular Forces

Beyond bonds within molecules, forces between molecules determine melting points, boiling points, and solubility:

| Force | Strength | Example |
|-------|----------|---------|
| **Hydrogen bonding** | Strong (for an intermolecular force) | Water (H₂O), DNA base pairs |
| **Dipole-dipole** | Moderate | HCl, acetone |
| **London dispersion** | Weak (but universal) | All molecules; dominant in nonpolar substances like oil |

Hydrogen bonding is why water has anomalously high boiling point for such a small molecule — and why ice floats (hydrogen bonds form an open crystal structure).

## Chemical Reactions

A chemical reaction rearranges atoms — breaking old bonds and forming new ones — without changing the atoms themselves.

### Types of Reactions

| Type | Pattern | Example |
|------|---------|---------|
| **Synthesis** | A + B → AB | 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O |
| **Decomposition** | AB → A + B | 2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂ |
| **Single replacement** | A + BC → AC + B | Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu |
| **Double replacement** | AB + CD → AD + CB | AgNO₃ + NaCl → AgCl + NaNO₃ |
| **Combustion** | Fuel + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O | CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O |
| **Redox** | Transfer of electrons between species | Rusting: 4Fe + 3O₂ → 2Fe₂O₃ |

### Balancing Equations

Every chemical equation must have the same number of each type of atom on both sides (conservation of mass). Coefficients (numbers in front of molecules) are adjusted to achieve balance — never the subscripts within a molecule.

### Reaction Rates

| Factor | Effect on Rate | Why |
|--------|---------------|-----|
| **Temperature** | Increases | Molecules move faster, collide more energetically |
| **Concentration** | Increases | More molecules per volume, more collisions |
| **Surface area** | Increases | More exposed particles available to react |
| **Catalyst** | Increases | Lowers activation energy without being consumed |
| **Pressure** (gases) | Increases | Molecules forced closer together |

## Acids, Bases, and pH

The pH scale runs from 0 to 14. Below 7 is acidic, above 7 is basic (alkaline), and 7 is neutral.

| Substance | pH | Notes |
|-----------|-----|-------|
| **Stomach acid** | ~1.5 | Strong enough to dissolve metal |
| **Lemon juice** | ~2.0 | Citric acid |
| **Vinegar** | ~2.5 | Acetic acid |
| **Pure water** | 7.0 | Neutral |
| **Blood** | ~7.4 | Tightly regulated; deviation of ±0.5 can be fatal |
| **Baking soda solution** | ~8.5 | Mild base |
| **Bleach** | ~13 | Strong base (sodium hydroxide) |

Each whole number step represents a 10-fold change in acidity, so pH 3 is ten times more acidic than pH 4 and a hundred times more acidic than pH 5.

## Organic Chemistry

Organic chemistry is the study of carbon-based compounds. Carbon is unique — it forms four stable covalent bonds, can bond to itself in long chains and rings, and is the basis of all known life.

### Key Functional Groups

| Group | Structure | Found In |
|-------|-----------|----------|
| **Hydroxyl** (−OH) | Oxygen + hydrogen | Alcohols (ethanol, methanol) |
| **Carbonyl** (C=O) | Carbon double-bonded to oxygen | Aldehydes, ketones, sugars |
| **Carboxyl** (−COOH) | Carbonyl + hydroxyl | Organic acids (acetic acid, amino acids) |
| **Amino** (−NH₂) | Nitrogen + two hydrogens | Amino acids, proteins |
| **Phosphate** (−PO₄) | Phosphorus + four oxygens | DNA, ATP (cellular energy) |
| **Methyl** (−CH₃) | Carbon + three hydrogens | Gene expression (methylation), fats |

### Macromolecules of Life

| Macromolecule | Building Block | Function |
|---------------|---------------|----------|
| **Proteins** | Amino acids (20 types) | Enzymes, structure, signaling, transport |
| **Carbohydrates** | Monosaccharides (glucose) | Energy storage, structural (cellulose) |
| **Lipids** | Fatty acids + glycerol | Cell membranes, long-term energy storage, hormones |
| **Nucleic acids** | Nucleotides | Information storage (DNA, RNA) |

## States of Matter

| State | Shape | Volume | Particle Behavior |
|-------|-------|--------|-------------------|
| **Solid** | Fixed | Fixed | Particles vibrate in place; strong bonds |
| **Liquid** | Takes container shape | Fixed | Particles slide past each other; moderate bonds |
| **Gas** | Fills container | Fills container | Particles move freely; weak/no bonds |
| **Plasma** | Fills container | Fills container | Ionized gas; electrons stripped from atoms (stars, lightning) |

Phase changes occur at specific temperatures and pressures: melting, freezing, evaporation, condensation, sublimation (solid → gas directly), and deposition (gas → solid).

## Summary

Chemistry is the science of how atoms combine and interact. The periodic table organizes elements by their chemical behavior, which is determined by electron configuration. Atoms bond ionically (transfer electrons), covalently (share electrons), or metallically (free-flowing electrons). Chemical reactions rearrange atoms while conserving mass, and their rates depend on temperature, concentration, surface area, and catalysts. Acids and bases are measured on the logarithmic pH scale. Organic chemistry — the study of carbon compounds — underpins all of biology through four macromolecule classes: proteins, carbohydrates, lipids, and nucleic acids. Understanding chemistry is essential for biology, medicine, materials science, environmental science, and engineering.
