# Science and Nature

Science is how we figure out what the universe is actually doing — not what we assume it is doing, not what ancient texts say it does, but what careful observation and experimentation reveal. The scientific method is simple in principle: observe something, form a hypothesis, test it, and revise your understanding based on the results. What makes science powerful is that it is self-correcting. Every claim is provisional — accepted only until better evidence comes along.

This file covers the major branches of natural science: physics, chemistry, biology, and the Earth and space sciences. Think of it as a map of the physical world.

---

## Physics — The Rules of the Game

Physics asks the most fundamental question in science: how does matter and energy behave? Everything else — chemistry, biology, engineering — builds on the answers.

### The Four Fundamental Forces

Every interaction in the universe comes down to four forces. Everything you have ever felt — the ground under your feet, the warmth of the sun, the magnet on your fridge — is one of these at work.

Gravity is absurdly weak compared to the others — a tiny fridge magnet overpowers the gravitational pull of the entire Earth. But gravity has infinite range and never cancels out, so on cosmic scales it dominates.

### Key Concepts

**Relativity**: Einstein showed that space and time are not separate — they form a single fabric called spacetime. Mass curves spacetime, and that curvature is what we experience as gravity. Special relativity gives us E = mc²: a small amount of mass contains an enormous amount of energy (which is how the sun shines and how nuclear weapons work).

**Quantum mechanics**: At the subatomic scale, the rules change completely. Particles can be in multiple states at once (superposition), can be entangled across distances (measuring one instantly affects the other), and behave as both waves and particles. Quantum mechanics is the most precisely tested theory in all of science, and it is deeply counterintuitive.

**Thermodynamics**: Three laws govern energy. First law: energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed. Second law: entropy (disorder) always increases — this is why coffee cools down, why engines are never 100% efficient, and why time has a direction. Third law: absolute zero (−273.15°C) is unreachable.

## Chemistry — How Atoms Combine

If physics is the rules of the game, chemistry is what happens when the pieces interact. Chemistry studies how atoms bond, break apart, and rearrange — which is to say, how all materials behave.

### The Atom in 30 Seconds

Atoms have a nucleus (protons + neutrons) surrounded by electrons in shells. The number of protons defines the element (1 = hydrogen, 6 = carbon, 79 = gold). The number of neutrons determines the isotope (carbon-12 vs. carbon-14). The arrangement of electrons determines how the atom bonds with other atoms.

### The Periodic Table

The periodic table is not a random list — it is a map of chemical behavior. Elements in the same column (group) behave similarly because they have the same number of valence electrons.

| Group | Elements | Behavior |
|-------|----------|----------|
| **Alkali metals** (Group 1) | Li, Na, K... | Extremely reactive, lose 1 electron, explode in water |
| **Halogens** (Group 17) | F, Cl, Br, I... | Very reactive, gain 1 electron, form salts |
| **Noble gases** (Group 18) | He, Ne, Ar... | Almost completely inert — full electron shells |
| **Transition metals** (Groups 3-12) | Fe, Cu, Au... | Variable oxidation states, form colored compounds |

### Chemical Bonding

Atoms bond to achieve stable electron configurations. The three main types:

- **Ionic bonds**: One atom gives up an electron, another accepts it. Opposite charges attract. Example: NaCl (table salt).
- **Covalent bonds**: Atoms share electrons. Stronger than ionic bonds. Example: H₂O (water), where oxygen shares electrons with two hydrogen atoms.
- **Metallic bonds**: Electrons flow freely between atoms, creating a "sea" of charge. This is why metals conduct electricity and heat.

### Acids, Bases, and pH

The pH scale runs from 0 to 14. Below 7 is acidic, above 7 is basic (alkaline), and 7 is neutral. Stomach acid sits around pH 1.5 — strong enough to dissolve metal. Pure water is pH 7. Bleach is pH 13. Each whole number step represents a 10-fold change in acidity, so pH 3 is ten times more acidic than pH 4 and a hundred times more acidic than pH 5.

## Biology — The Study of Living Things

Biology is the science of life. All living things share certain features: they are made of cells, they use energy, they grow, they respond to their environment, they reproduce, and they evolve.

### Cells — The Basic Unit

Every living thing is made of cells. There are two fundamental types:

| Feature | Prokaryotic | Eukaryotic |
|---------|-------------|------------|
| **Nucleus** | No — DNA floats freely | Yes — DNA enclosed in membrane |
| **Size** | Small (1-5 μm) | Larger (10-100 μm) |
| **Examples** | Bacteria, archaea | Animals, plants, fungi, protists |
| **Complexity** | Simple | Complex internal structures (organelles) |
| **Reproduction** | Binary fission | Mitosis or meiosis |

### DNA and Genetics

DNA is the instruction manual for building and running a living organism. It is a double helix made of four bases: adenine (A), thymine (T), cytosine (C), and guanine (G). A always pairs with T; C always pairs with G. This pairing is how DNA copies itself.

Genes are sections of DNA that code for proteins. The human genome contains roughly 20,000 genes packed into 23 pairs of chromosomes. Mutations — changes in the DNA sequence — are the raw material of evolution. Most mutations are harmless, some are beneficial, and a few cause disease.

### Evolution by Natural Selection

Evolution is not "just a theory" in the colloquial sense — it is the unifying framework of all biology. The mechanism is straightforward:

1. Organisms produce more offspring than the environment can support.
2. Individuals vary in their traits (some traits are heritable).
3. Individuals with traits better suited to the environment are more likely to survive and reproduce.
4. Over generations, advantageous traits become more common in the population.

This process — without any guiding intelligence — explains the diversity of all life on Earth, from bacteria to blue whales.

## Electricity and Magnetism

Electricity and magnetism are two faces of the same force: electromagnetism. Moving charges create magnetic fields; changing magnetic fields create electric currents. This relationship powers most of modern technology.

| Concept | Symbol | Unit | What It Means |
|---------|--------|------|---------------|
| **Voltage** | V | Volts (V) | The "pressure" pushing charges through a circuit |
| **Current** | I | Amperes (A) | How many charges flow past a point per second |
| **Resistance** | R | Ohms (Ω) | How much a material opposes the flow of current |
| **Power** | P | Watts (W) | Rate of energy transfer (P = V × I) |

**Ohm's Law** (V = I × R) is the foundation of circuit analysis. If you know any two values, you can calculate the third.

**Electromagnetic induction** — discovered by Faraday — is how power plants generate electricity. Spin a magnet inside a coil of wire, and you get current. That is essentially what every turbine does, whether driven by steam (coal, nuclear), water (hydroelectric), or wind.

## Earth Science and Cosmology

### Earth's Structure

The planet is layered like an onion:

| Layer | Depth | Composition | State |
|-------|-------|-------------|-------|
| **Crust** | 0-70 km | Silicate rocks | Solid (rigid plates) |
| **Upper mantle** | 70-410 km | Peridotite | Semi-solid (flows slowly) |
| **Lower mantle** | 410-2,900 km | Denser silicates | Solid under pressure |
| **Outer core** | 2,900-5,150 km | Iron + nickel | Liquid (generates magnetic field) |
| **Inner core** | 5,150-6,371 km | Iron + nickel | Solid (extreme pressure) |

Tectonic plates — chunks of the crust and upper mantle — drift a few centimeters per year on the flowing mantle beneath them. When plates collide, you get mountains (the Himalayas). When they pull apart, you get rift valleys and volcanoes (Iceland). When they slide past each other, you get earthquakes (the San Andreas Fault).

### The Universe

- **Age**: ~13.8 billion years (from the Big Bang)
- **Observable diameter**: ~93 billion light-years
- **Galaxies**: ~2 trillion in the observable universe
- **Stars in the Milky Way**: 100-400 billion
- **Nearest star**: Proxima Centauri, 4.24 light-years away
- **Composition**: ~5% ordinary matter, ~27% dark matter, ~68% dark energy

Dark matter and dark energy are the biggest unsolved problems in physics. We can measure their effects (galaxies rotate too fast for the visible matter alone; the universe's expansion is accelerating), but we have no idea what they actually are.

## Energy

Energy comes in many forms — kinetic (motion), potential (stored), thermal (heat), chemical, electrical, nuclear — but it always obeys one rule: it cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed. This is the first law of thermodynamics, and it is absolute.

The second law is less cheerful: every energy transformation loses some energy as waste heat. No process is 100% efficient. A car engine converts about 25% of gasoline energy into motion; the rest becomes heat and sound. A light bulb converts about 5% of electricity into visible light (the old incandescent ones — LEDs are better at ~40%).

| Energy Source | Type | Efficiency | Key Limitation |
|---------------|------|-----------|----------------|
| **Coal** | Non-renewable | ~33-40% | CO₂ emissions, air pollution |
| **Natural gas** | Non-renewable | ~40-60% (combined cycle) | Methane leaks, still emits CO₂ |
| **Nuclear fission** | Non-renewable (fuel-limited) | ~33-37% | Waste storage, public opposition |
| **Solar PV** | Renewable | ~15-22% | Intermittent, requires storage |
| **Wind** | Renewable | ~35-45% | Intermittent, location-dependent |
| **Hydroelectric** | Renewable | ~85-90% | Geography-limited, ecosystem impact |

## Summary

Science is not a collection of facts — it is a method for finding reliable knowledge about the natural world. Physics gives us the fundamental rules. Chemistry explains how atoms combine. Biology shows how those combinations produce life. Earth science and cosmology place our planet in the larger context of the universe. What unifies all of these is the commitment to evidence over assumption, and the willingness to revise our understanding when the evidence demands it.
