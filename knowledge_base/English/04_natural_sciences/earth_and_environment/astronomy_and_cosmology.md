# Astronomy and Cosmology

Astronomy is the study of celestial objects — stars, planets, galaxies, and everything beyond Earth's atmosphere. Cosmology is the study of the universe as a whole: its origin, structure, evolution, and ultimate fate. Together, they address some of the most fundamental questions humans have ever asked: How did the universe begin? How big is it? Are we alone? What will happen to it in the end?

---

## The Scale of the Universe

### Cosmic Distance Ladder

| Method | Range | What It Measures |
|--------|-------|-----------------|
| **Radar** | Solar system | Bounce radio signals off planets; measure return time |
| **Parallax** | Up to ~10,000 light-years | Apparent shift of nearby stars against distant background as Earth orbits the Sun |
| **Standard candles (Cepheid variables)** | Up to ~100 million light-years | Pulsation period correlates with intrinsic brightness |
| **Type Ia supernovae** | Billions of light-years | Exploding white dwarfs with consistent peak luminosity |
| **Redshift (Hubble's law)** | Cosmological distances | Recession velocity proportional to distance; expanding universe |

### Mind-Bending Numbers

| Quantity | Value |
|----------|-------|
| **Age of the universe** | ~13.8 billion years |
| **Observable universe diameter** | ~93 billion light-years |
| **Number of galaxies (observable)** | ~2 trillion |
| **Number of stars in the Milky Way** | ~100–400 billion |
| **Distance to the nearest star (Proxima Centauri)** | 4.24 light-years |
| **Mass of the Sun** | ~2 × 10^30 kg |
| **Speed of light** | ~300,000 km/s |

## Stars

### Stellar Lifecycle

| Stage | Description | Duration |
|-------|-------------|----------|
| **Molecular cloud** | Cold, dense gas and dust; gravity begins to collapse regions | Millions of years |
| **Protostar** | Collapsing core heats up; not yet fusing hydrogen | ~100,000 years |
| **Main sequence** | Hydrogen fusion in the core; stable; where our Sun is now | ~10 billion years (Sun-like) |
| **Red giant** | Core hydrogen exhausted; outer layers expand and cool | ~1 billion years |
| **Planetary nebula / supernova** | Outer layers ejected (low mass) or core collapses (high mass) | Days to thousands of years |
| **Remnant** | White dwarf, neutron star, or black hole | Billions of years or longer |

### Stellar Classification (OBAFGKM)

| Class | Colour | Temperature | Example |
|-------|--------|-------------|---------|
| **O** | Blue | >30,000 K | Mintaka |
| **B** | Blue-white | 10,000–30,000 K | Rigel |
| **A** | White | 7,500–10,000 K | Sirius, Vega |
| **F** | Yellow-white | 6,000–7,500 K | Procyon |
| **G** | Yellow | 5,200–6,000 K | Sun; Alpha Centauri A |
| **K** | Orange | 3,700–5,200 K | Arcturus; Alpha Centauri B |
| **M** | Red | 2,400–3,700 K | Betelgeuse; Proxima Centauri |

## Galaxies

### Types of Galaxies (Hubble Classification)

| Type | Description | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Elliptical (E)** | Smooth; featureless; oval shape; old stars; little gas | M87 |
| **Spiral (S)** | Flat disc; spiral arms; bulge at centre; active star formation | Milky Way; Andromeda (M31) |
| **Barred spiral (SB)** | Spiral with a bar through the centre | NGC 1300 |
| **Lenticular (S0)** | Disc without spiral arms; intermediate between elliptical and spiral | NGC 2787 |
| **Irregular (Irr)** | No defined shape; often result of galaxy interactions | Large Magellanic Cloud |

### The Milky Way

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| **Type** | Barred spiral (SBbc) |
| **Diameter** | ~100,000 light-years |
| **Number of stars** | ~200–400 billion |
| **Mass** | ~1.5 trillion solar masses (including dark matter) |
| **Age** | ~13.6 billion years |
| **Central black hole** | Sagittarius A* (~4 million solar masses) |
| **Sun's position** | ~26,000 light-years from centre; in the Orion Arm |

## Cosmology

### The Big Bang Theory

| Evidence | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB)** | Afterglow of the Big Bang; 2.725 K; uniform in all directions; tiny fluctuations seeded galaxies |
| **Hubble expansion** | Galaxies are receding from us; the further away, the faster — space itself is expanding |
| **Abundance of light elements** | Big Bang nucleosynthesis predicts the observed ratios of hydrogen, helium, and lithium |
| **Large-scale structure** | Distribution of galaxies matches predictions from initial density fluctuations |

### Timeline of the Universe

| Time After Big Bang | Event |
|---------------------|-------|
| **10^-43 seconds** | Planck epoch; all forces unified; physics as we know it breaks down |
| **10^-36 seconds** | Inflation begins; universe expands exponentially |
| **10^-12 seconds** | Electroweak symmetry breaks; Higgs field gives particles mass |
| **10^-6 seconds** | Quarks combine into protons and neutrons |
| **3 minutes** | Big Bang nucleosynthesis; first atomic nuclei (H, He, Li) |
| **380,000 years** | Recombination; atoms form; CMB released; universe becomes transparent |
| **100–200 million years** | First stars ignite (Population III stars) |
| **1 billion years** | First galaxies form |
| **9.2 billion years** | Solar system forms |
| **13.8 billion years** | Now |

### Dark Matter and Dark Energy

| Component | Proportion | Evidence | Nature |
|-----------|-----------|----------|--------|
| **Ordinary matter** | ~5% | Visible; emits/absorbs light | Well understood (atoms, particles) |
| **Dark matter** | ~27% | Galaxy rotation curves; gravitational lensing; CMB patterns | Doesn't interact with light; detected only through gravity |
| **Dark energy** | ~68% | Accelerating expansion of the universe (Type Ia supernovae) | Unknown; possibly cosmological constant (vacuum energy) |

## Exoplanets and the Search for Life

### Detection Methods

| Method | How It Works | Strengths |
|--------|-------------|-----------|
| **Transit** | Planet passes in front of star; star dims slightly | Most productive method (Kepler, TESS); gives size and orbital period |
| **Radial velocity** | Planet's gravity causes star to wobble; Doppler shift in star's light | Gives mass; works for massive planets close to star |
| **Direct imaging** | Block star's light; photograph the planet directly | Works for large planets far from star |
| **Gravitational microlensing** | Planet's gravity bends light from a background star | Detects planets at great distances; rare events |
| **Astrometry** | Measure star's precise position; detect wobble | Complementary to radial velocity |

### The Habitable Zone

| Factor | Requirement |
|--------|-------------|
| **Distance from star** | Not too hot; not too cold; liquid water possible on surface |
| **Star type** | Stable; long-lived (G, K, M dwarfs preferred) |
| **Planet mass** | Enough gravity to hold an atmosphere; not so massive as to become a gas giant |
| **Atmosphere** | Greenhouse effect to maintain surface temperature; protection from radiation |
| **Magnetic field** | Deflects stellar wind; protects atmosphere |

## Summary

Astronomy and cosmology study everything from planets to the entire universe. Stars are born in molecular clouds, spend billions of years fusing hydrogen, and end as white dwarfs, neutron stars, or black holes. Galaxies — spiral, elliptical, irregular — are the building blocks of the large-scale structure of the universe. The Big Bang theory, supported by the CMB, Hubble expansion, and light element abundances, describes a universe that began 13.8 billion years ago and is composed of ~5% ordinary matter, ~27% dark matter, and ~68% dark energy. Exoplanet discoveries have shown that planets are common, and the search for habitable worlds continues. The overarching lesson is how much we don't know: dark matter and dark energy together make up 95% of the universe, and we still don't know what either of them is.
