# Earth Science

Earth science studies the planet we live on — its structure, processes, history, and the systems that make it habitable. It spans geology (the solid Earth), oceanography (the oceans), meteorology (the atmosphere and weather), and climatology (long-term climate patterns). What unifies these fields is the understanding that Earth is a dynamic system: its interior is constantly moving, its surface is being reshaped, and its atmosphere and oceans interact in complex ways.

---

## Earth's Internal Structure

The planet is layered like an onion. Each layer has distinct properties determined by temperature, pressure, and composition.

| Layer | Depth | Composition | State | Key Feature |
|-------|-------|-------------|-------|-------------|
| **Crust** | 0-70 km | Silicate rocks | Solid (rigid plates) | Oceanic crust (thin, dense, basalt) vs continental crust (thick, lighter, granite) |
| **Upper mantle** | 70-410 km | Peridotite | Semi-solid (flows slowly) | Contains the asthenosphere — the layer plates glide on |
| **Lower mantle** | 410-2,900 km | Denser silicates | Solid under pressure | Convection currents drive plate movement |
| **Outer core** | 2,900-5,150 km | Iron + nickel | Liquid | Generates Earth's magnetic field through convection |
| **Inner core** | 5,150-6,371 km | Iron + nickel | Solid (extreme pressure) | About the size of the Moon; temperature ~5,400°C (similar to the Sun's surface) |

### The Magnetic Field

Earth's magnetic field is generated by the motion of liquid iron in the outer core (the geodynamo). It extends tens of thousands of kilometers into space and protects the planet from the solar wind — a stream of charged particles from the Sun. Without it, the solar wind would gradually strip away the atmosphere (which is likely what happened to Mars).

The magnetic poles are not fixed — they wander and occasionally flip entirely (geomagnetic reversals), happening roughly every 200,000-300,000 years. The last reversal was about 780,000 years ago.

## Plate Tectonics

Plate tectonics is the unifying theory of geology. Earth's crust is broken into about 15 major tectonic plates that drift a few centimeters per year on the flowing mantle beneath them.

### Types of Plate Boundaries

| Boundary Type | Plates | What Happens | Example |
|---------------|--------|-------------|---------|
| **Convergent** | Moving together | Collision → mountains (continent-continent) or subduction → volcanoes and trenches (ocean-continent) | Himalayas (India-Eurasia); Andes (Nazca-South America) |
| **Divergent** | Moving apart | Magma rises → new crust forms; rift valleys or mid-ocean ridges | Mid-Atlantic Ridge; East African Rift; Iceland |
| **Transform** | Sliding past each other | Earthquakes along faults; no new crust created | San Andreas Fault (California) |

### Evidence for Plate Tectonics

| Evidence | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **Continental fit** | South America and Africa fit together like puzzle pieces |
| **Fossil distribution** | Same fossils found on continents now separated by oceans |
| **Rock matching** | Mountain ranges and rock formations match across oceans |
| **Paleomagnetism** | Magnetic stripes on the ocean floor record polarity reversals and show seafloor spreading |
| **Earthquake patterns** | Earthquakes cluster along plate boundaries |
| **Hotspot chains** | Volcanic island chains (Hawaii) record plate movement over stationary mantle plumes |

### The Rock Cycle

Rocks are constantly being transformed between three types:

| Rock Type | How It Forms | Example |
|-----------|-------------|---------|
| **Igneous** | Cooling of magma or lava | Granite (slow cooling underground); basalt (fast cooling on surface) |
| **Sedimentary** | Compression of sediments (sand, mud, shells) | Sandstone; limestone; shale |
| **Metamorphic** | Existing rock transformed by heat and pressure | Marble (from limestone); slate (from shale) |

Any rock type can become any other type given enough time and the right conditions. Igneous rock can be weathered into sediment → compressed into sedimentary rock → buried and heated → metamorphic rock → melted → igneous rock again.

|---------|-----------------|-----------------|-------------|

| **Diamond** | C | 10 | Kimberlite pipes; formed deep in Earth's mantle |

## The Atmosphere

Earth's atmosphere is a thin layer of gas held by gravity. It is divided into layers based on temperature patterns.

### Atmospheric Layers

| Layer | Altitude | Temperature Trend | Key Feature |
|-------|----------|-------------------|-------------|
| **Troposphere** | 0-12 km | Decreases with altitude | Where weather happens; contains ~75% of atmospheric mass |
| **Stratosphere** | 12-50 km | Increases with altitude | Contains the ozone layer (absorbs UV radiation) |
| **Mesosphere** | 50-85 km | Decreases with altitude | Meteors burn up here |
| **Thermosphere** | 85-600 km | Increases with altitude | Aurora borealis; International Space Station orbits here |
| **Exosphere** | 600-10,000 km | Gradually fades to space | Transition to outer space |

### Atmospheric Composition

| Gas | Percentage | Role |
|-----|-----------|------|
| **Nitrogen (N₂)** | 78.08% | Dilutes oxygen; essential for proteins (fixed by bacteria) |
| **Oxygen (O₂)** | 20.95% | Respiration; combustion; produced by photosynthesis |
| **Argon (Ar)** | 0.93% | Inert; industrial uses (welding, light bulbs) |
| **CO₂** | ~0.04% (420 ppm) | Greenhouse gas; photosynthesis; rising due to fossil fuels |
| **Water vapor** | 0-4% (variable) | Greenhouse gas; drives weather and clouds |

## Weather and Climate

Weather is short-term atmospheric conditions; climate is the long-term average. The key driver of both is the Sun's uneven heating of Earth's surface — the equator receives more energy than the poles, and the atmosphere and oceans work to redistribute that heat.

### Weather Systems

| Phenomenon | How It Forms |
|------------|-------------|
| **Wind** | Air moves from high to low pressure; Coriolis effect curves its path |
| **Rain** | Warm air rises → cools → water vapor condenses → droplets fall |
| **Hurricanes** | Warm ocean water (>26°C) + low pressure + Coriolis → rotating storm systems |
| **Tornadoes** | Severe thunderstorms with wind shear → rotating mesocyclone → funnel cloud |
| **Fronts** | Boundary between warm and cold air masses → clouds, precipitation |

### Ocean Currents

The oceans distribute heat around the planet through a system of surface and deep currents:

| Current | Type | Effect |
|---------|------|--------|
| **Gulf Stream** | Surface (warm) | Carries warm water from Gulf of Mexico to Europe; moderates European climate |
| **Thermohaline circulation** | Deep (global "conveyor belt") | Driven by temperature and salinity differences; takes ~1,000 years for a full cycle |
| **El Niño / La Niña** | Surface (Pacific) | Periodic warming/cooling of equatorial Pacific; affects global weather patterns |
| **Upwelling** | Deep → surface | Brings nutrient-rich water to surface; supports major fisheries |

## Geological Time

Earth is about 4.54 billion years old. The vastness of geological time is hard to comprehend. If Earth's history were compressed into a single 24-hour day:

| Time | Event |
|------|-------|
| **00:00** | Earth forms |
| **04:00** | Earliest evidence of life (stromatolites) |
| **20:00** | First multicellular organisms |
| **21:00** | Cambrian explosion — rapid diversification of complex life |
| **22:30** | First land plants |
| **23:00** | Age of dinosaurs begins |
| **23:40** | Asteroid impact kills dinosaurs |
| **23:55** | First hominids |
| **23:59:56** | Modern humans appear |
| **23:59:59** | Recorded history begins |

### Major Extinction Events

| Event | When | Severity | Cause |
|-------|------|----------|-------|
| **Ordovician-Silurian** | ~445 million years ago | ~85% species lost | Glaciation, sea level drop |
| **Late Devonian** | ~375 million years ago | ~75% | Climate change, ocean anoxia |
| **Permian-Triassic ("The Great Dying")** | ~252 million years ago | ~96% | Massive volcanism, climate collapse |
| **Triassic-Jurassic** | ~201 million years ago | ~80% | Volcanism, climate change |
| **Cretaceous-Paleogene** | ~66 million years ago | ~76% | Asteroid impact (Chicxulub); volcanism |

## Summary

Earth science reveals a planet in constant motion. The interior is layered — a solid inner core, liquid outer core generating the magnetic field, a convecting mantle, and a thin rigid crust broken into tectonic plates. Plate tectonics shapes the surface: mountains rise where plates collide, oceans widen where they diverge, and earthquakes mark where they slide past each other. The atmosphere — mostly nitrogen and oxygen — protects life from radiation and distributes heat through weather systems and ocean currents. Climate is the long-term pattern, driven by solar energy, greenhouse gases, and ocean circulation. Earth's 4.54-billion-year history includes five mass extinctions, the most recent of which cleared the way for mammals — and eventually humans — to dominate. Understanding Earth as a system is essential for addressing the environmental challenges of the 21st century.
