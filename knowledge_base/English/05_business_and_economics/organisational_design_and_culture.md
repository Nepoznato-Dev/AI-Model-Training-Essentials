# Organisational Design and Culture

Organisational design is the deliberate arrangement of structure, processes, roles, and decision rights to execute strategy effectively. Culture is the shared values, beliefs, norms, and behaviours that shape how people actually work together. Structure provides the framework; culture shapes behaviour. A structure can be redesigned on paper, but if the culture does not support the change, implementation will fail. Similarly, a strong culture paired with an ineffective structure will gradually undermine organisational performance. The most effective organisations address both — and recognise that they are deeply interconnected.

---

## Organisational Structures

### Common Structures

| Structure | Description | Strengths | Weaknesses |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|------------|
| **Functional** | Grouped by specialisation (engineering, marketing, sales) | Deep expertise; efficiency; clear career paths | Silos; slow cross-functional coordination |
| **Divisional** | Grouped by product, market, or geography | Focus on customer/market; P&L accountability | Duplication of functions; resource competition |
| **Matrix** | Dual reporting: functional + product/project | Flexibility; cross-functional collaboration | Confusion; power struggles; slow decisions |
| **Flat / horizontal** | Few management layers; broad spans of control | Fast decisions; empowered employees; low overhead | Hard to scale; unclear reporting |
| **Team-based** | Organised around cross-functional teams | Autonomy; speed; customer focus | Coordination across teams; consistency |
| **Network / platform** | Core team + external partners; platform enables others | Scalability; flexibility; asset-light | Quality control; dependency on partners |
| **Holacracy** | Self-managed teams; roles defined by accountabilities | High autonomy; adaptive | Confusing; doesn't work for everyone |

### Span of Control

| Factor | Narrow Span (4-6) | Wide Span (10-15+) |
|--------|-------------------|---------------------|
| **Management layers** | Many | Few |
| **Supervision** | Close; hands-on | Delegated; hands-off |
| **Best for** | Complex tasks; inexperienced teams | Routine tasks; experienced, autonomous teams |
| **Risk** | Micromanagement; slow decisions | Lack of support; coordination gaps |

### Centralisation vs Decentralisation

| Dimension | Centralised | Decentralised |
|-----------|-----------|---------------|
| **Decision-making** | Top-down | Pushed to the edges |
| **Speed** | Slow (bottleneck at top) | Fast (decisions made locally) |
| **Consistency** | High | Variable |
| **Innovation** | Limited by top's bandwidth | Emerges from multiple sources |
| **Best for** | Crisis; early-stage; compliance-heavy industries | Knowledge work; fast-moving markets; scale-ups |

## Design Principles

### Key Decisions in Organisational Design

| Decision | Question | Options |
|----------|----------|---------|
| **Departmentalisation** | How do we group people? | By function; product; customer; geography; process |
| **Chain of command** | Who reports to whom? | Clear hierarchy; matrix; network |
| **Decision rights** | Who decides what? | Centralised; decentralised; RACI matrix |
| **Coordination mechanisms** | How do teams work together? | Liaison roles; cross-functional teams; integrators; shared goals |
| **Standardisation** | How much is standardised vs local? | Processes; outputs; skills; norms |

### Galbraith's Star Model

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Strategy** | Direction; goals; competitive advantage; the reason the organisation exists |
| **Structure** | Reporting relationships; hierarchy; grouping of units |
| **Processes** | Information flow; decision-making; planning; budgeting |
| **Rewards** | Incentives; performance measurement; alignment with strategy |
| **People** | Hiring; development; culture; the human capabilities |

The key insight: strategy drives structure. All other elements must align. Changing structure without changing processes, rewards, and people development will fail.

## Organisational Culture

### What Culture Actually Is

| Layer | Description | Visibility |
|-------|-------------|-----------|
| **Artifacts** | Visible structures, processes, behaviours, dress code, office layout | Easy to see; hard to interpret |
| **Espoused values** | Stated strategies, goals, philosophies, mission statements | Publicly declared; may not match actual behaviour |
| **Basic assumptions** | Unconscious, taken-for-granted beliefs, perceptions, thoughts, feelings | Invisible; the real driver of behaviour |

### Culture Types (Competing Values Framework)

| Culture | Focus | Characteristics | Example |
|---------|-------|----------------|---------|
| **Clan** (collaborate) | Internal + flexible | Family-like; mentoring; teamwork; consensus | Zappos; small startups |
| **Adhocracy** (create) | External + flexible | Innovative; risk-taking; entrepreneurial; dynamic | Google (early); SpaceX |
| **Market** (compete) | External + controlled | Results-oriented; competitive; goal-focused; market share | Amazon; Wall Street firms |
| **Hierarchy** (control) | Internal + controlled | Structured; process-driven; efficiency; consistency | Government agencies; military; traditional banks |

### Cultural Dimensions

| Dimension | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Psychological safety** | Can people take risks and be vulnerable without fear of punishment? |
| **Accountability** | Are people held responsible for their commitments? |
| **Transparency** | Is information shared openly or hoarded as power? |
| **Innovation tolerance** | Is failure punished or treated as learning? |
| **Customer orientation** | Do decisions start from customer needs or internal convenience? |
| **Speed vs quality** | Is the bias toward fast iteration or careful deliberation? |
| **Hierarchy respect** | Can junior people challenge senior people's ideas? |

## Change Management

### Why Change Initiatives Fail

| Reason | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **No urgency** | People don't see why change is necessary |
| **No coalition** | No powerful group driving the change |
| **No vision** | Unclear what the future state looks like |
| **Poor communication** | People don't understand the change or their role in it |
| **No quick wins** | No visible progress to build momentum |
| **Premature celebration** | Declaring victory before change is embedded |
| **Culture resistance** | Change conflicts with deep-seated assumptions |
| **No reinforcement** | Old behaviours rewarded; new behaviours not sustained |

### Kotter's 8-Step Model

| Step | Action |
|------|--------|
| **1. Create urgency** | Help people see the threat of not changing |
| **2. Build a guiding coalition** | Assemble a group with enough power and credibility |
| **3. Form a strategic vision** | Create a clear picture of the future |
| **4. Enlist a volunteer army** | Get buy-in from a critical mass |
| **5. Enable action** | Remove barriers; empower people |
| **6. Generate short-term wins** | Create visible successes |
| **7. Sustain acceleration** | Keep pushing; don't declare victory too early |
| **8. Institute change** | Anchor new behaviours in culture |

## Remote and Hybrid Work

| Challenge | Strategy |
|-----------|----------|
| **Communication** | Default to written; async-first; document decisions |
| **Trust** | Measure outcomes, not hours; avoid surveillance |
| **Onboarding** | Structured programmes; buddy systems; explicit documentation |
| **Culture** | Intentional rituals; regular all-hands; virtual social events |
| **Equity** | Proximity bias: ensure remote workers get equal visibility and opportunities |
| **Collaboration** | Clear ownership; defined interfaces; regular syncs |
| **Wellbeing** | Respect boundaries; right to disconnect; avoid meeting overload |

## Summary

Organisational design and culture determine whether a company can execute its strategy. Structure defines reporting relationships, decision rights, and coordination mechanisms. Culture defines how people actually behave — the assumptions, norms, and values that guide daily work. Galbraith's Star Model reminds us that strategy, structure, processes, rewards, and people must all align. Culture types range from clan (collaborative) to adhocracy (innovative) to market (competitive) to hierarchy (controlled). Change management frameworks like Kotter's 8-step model provide structure for transformation, but culture is the real battleground — if the change conflicts with deep assumptions, it will fail regardless of how well it's planned. Remote and hybrid work add new challenges around communication, trust, and proximity bias. The best organisations design deliberately, build cultures that support their strategy, and remain willing to evolve both when the environment changes.
