# Intellectual Property and Innovation

Intellectual property (IP) refers to creations of the mind — inventions, artistic works, brand names, designs, trade secrets — that the law protects from unauthorised use. Innovation is the process of turning ideas into value. IP law exists to balance two competing interests: rewarding creators (by giving them temporary monopolies) and benefiting society (by ensuring knowledge eventually becomes freely available). Understanding IP is essential for anyone building products, creating content, running a business, or working in research.

---

## Types of Intellectual Property

### Overview

| Type | What It Protects | Duration | How Acquired | Example |
|------|-----------------|----------|-------------|---------|
| **Patent** | Inventions (processes, machines, compositions) | 20 years from filing | Application; examination; grant | A new drug compound; a manufacturing process |
| **Copyright** | Original works of authorship | Life + 70 years (most countries) | Automatic upon creation | Books; music; software code; films |
| **Trademark** | Brand identifiers (names, logos, slogans) | Indefinite (if used and renewed) | Use in commerce; registration strengthens | "Nike" name and swoosh logo |
| **Trade secret** | Confidential business information | Indefinite (as long as secret) | No registration; must take reasonable steps to keep secret | Coca-Cola formula; Google search algorithm |
| **Industrial design** | Visual appearance of a product | 15–25 years (varies) | Registration | Shape of a smartphone; furniture design |
| **Geographical indication** | Products with a specific geographical origin | Indefinite | Registration; linked to region | Champagne; Parmigiano-Reggiano; Darjeeling tea |

## Patents

### Patent Requirements

| Requirement | Description |
|-------------|-------------|
| **Novelty** | The invention must be new; not previously disclosed anywhere in the world |
| **Non-obviousness (inventive step)** | The invention must not be obvious to a person skilled in the relevant field |
| **Utility (industrial applicability)** | The invention must have a specific, practical use |
| **Subject matter eligibility** | Must be a process, machine, manufacture, or composition of matter; abstract ideas, laws of nature, and natural phenomena are not patentable |

### Types of Patents

| Type | Description | Term |
|------|-------------|------|
| **Utility patent** | Protects the function of an invention | 20 years from filing |
| **Design patent** | Protects the ornamental design of a functional item | 15 years from grant (US) |
| **Plant patent** | Protects new varieties of plants (asexually reproduced) | 20 years from filing |

### Patent Process

| Step | Description | Timeline |
|------|-------------|----------|
| **1. Prior art search** | Search existing patents and publications to check novelty | Weeks to months |
| **2. Draft application** | Write claims (define the scope of protection) and description | Weeks to months |
| **3. File application** | Submit to patent office (USPTO, EPO, etc.) | Day of filing = priority date |
| **4. Examination** | Patent examiner reviews for novelty, non-obviousness, utility | 1–5 years |
| **5. Office actions** | Examiner may reject; applicant argues or amends claims | Back-and-forth negotiation |
| **6. Grant** | If allowed, patent is granted; maintenance fees required | Periodic fees to keep alive |

## Copyright

### What Copyright Protects

| Protected | Not Protected |
|-----------|---------------|
| Literary works (books, articles, code) | Ideas; facts; systems |
| Musical works (melody, lyrics) | Titles; names; short phrases |
| Artistic works (paintings, photos, designs) | Methods of operation |
| Software code (as written) | The algorithm's function (may be patentable) |
| Films; TV shows; videos | Styles; techniques |
| Architectural designs | Functional aspects of buildings |

### Key Copyright Concepts

| Concept | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Fixation** | Copyright exists when the work is fixed in a tangible medium (written down, recorded) |
| **Automatic protection** | No registration required (but registration enables statutory damages in the US) |
| **Fair use / fair dealing** | Limited use without permission for criticism, commentary, education, parody |
| **Creative Commons** | Standardised licenses allowing various degrees of reuse |
| **Public domain** | Works not protected by copyright (expired, waived, or ineligible) |
| **Work for hire** | Works created by employees within their job scope belong to the employer |

## Trademarks

### Trademark Fundamentals

| Aspect | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Purpose** | Identify the source of goods/services; prevent consumer confusion |
| **Distinctiveness spectrum** | Fanciful (strongest) → Arbitrary → Suggestive → Descriptive (weak) → Generic (not protectable) |
| **Registration** | Not required (common law rights from use) but provides significant legal advantages |
| **Classes** | Goods/services classified into 45 classes (Nice Classification) |
| **Maintenance** | Must be used in commerce; registration renewed periodically |
| **Infringement** | Unauthorised use likely to cause confusion |

### Trademark Distinctiveness

| Category | Description | Protectability | Example |
|----------|-------------|---------------|---------|
| **Fanciful** | Invented words with no meaning outside the brand | Strongest | "Kodak"; "Xerox" |
| **Arbitrary** | Real words used unrelated to the product | Very strong | "Apple" for computers |
| **Suggestive** | Hints at the product's nature | Strong | "Netflix" (internet flicks) |
| **Descriptive** | Describes the product | Weak; requires secondary meaning | "Holiday Inn" |
| **Generic** | The common name of the product | Not protectable | "Aspirin" (was once a trademark) |

## Trade Secrets

| Aspect | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Definition** | Information that has economic value from being secret; is not generally known; and is subject to reasonable efforts to maintain secrecy |
| **Examples** | Formulas; algorithms; customer lists; manufacturing processes; business strategies |
| **Protection** | No registration; requires NDAs; access controls; need-to-know basis |
| **Duration** | Indefinite — as long as the secret is maintained |
| **Misappropriation** | Acquisition by improper means (theft, espionage, breach of NDA) |
| **vs Patents** | No disclosure required; no time limit; but no protection against independent discovery or reverse engineering |

## Open Source and Open Innovation

### Open Source Licenses

| License | Type | Key Requirement | Example Users |
|---------|------|----------------|---------------|
| **MIT** | Permissive | Include copyright notice; no warranty | jQuery; React |
| **Apache 2.0** | Permissive | Include notice; patent grant | Android; Kubernetes |
| **BSD (2-clause)** | Permissive | Include copyright notice | FreeBSD |
| **GPL v3** | Copyleft | Derivative works must also be GPL | Linux kernel; WordPress |
| **LGPL** | Weak copyleft | Linking doesn't trigger copyleft; modifying does | GTK |
| **MPL 2.0** | File-level copyleft | Modified files must be open; new files can be proprietary | Firefox |
| **AGPL** | Strong copyleft | Network use triggers source disclosure | MongoDB (formerly) |

### Open Innovation Models

| Model | Description | Example |
|-------|-------------|---------|
| **Open source** | Code freely available; community contributions | Linux; Kubernetes; React |
| **Open data** | Data shared publicly for anyone to use | Government datasets; research data |
| **Open hardware** | Hardware designs shared openly | Arduino; Raspberry Pi |
| **Patent pools** | Multiple companies share patents in a domain | MPEG-LA (video codecs) |
| **Pre-competitive collaboration** | Competitors collaborate on shared challenges | Semiconductor Research Corporation |

## IP Strategy

### When to Use Each Type of IP

| Situation | Recommended IP | Reason |
|-----------|---------------|--------|
| **Novel invention** | Patent | 20-year monopoly; strong protection |
| **Software algorithm** | Patent + trade secret | Patent if patentable; keep implementation secret |
| **Brand name / logo** | Trademark | Protects brand identity indefinitely |
| **Written content / code** | Copyright | Automatic; protects expression |
| **Recipe / formula** | Trade secret | No disclosure; indefinite protection |
| **Product design** | Design patent + trade dress | Protects appearance |
| **Fast-moving market** | Trade secret + first-mover advantage | Patents take too long; move faster than competitors can copy |

## Summary

Intellectual property law balances rewarding creators with benefiting society. Patents protect inventions for 20 years in exchange for public disclosure. Copyright protects original works of authorship for the creator's life plus 70 years. Trademarks protect brand identifiers indefinitely as long as they're used. Trade secrets protect confidential information indefinitely but offer no protection against independent discovery. Open source licenses provide an alternative to traditional IP, enabling collaborative development. The right IP strategy depends on the type of creation, the market, the speed of the industry, and the company's goals. Understanding IP is not just for lawyers — it's essential for anyone creating, building, or innovating in a knowledge economy.
