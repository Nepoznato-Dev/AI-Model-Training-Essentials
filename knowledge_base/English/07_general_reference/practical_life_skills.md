# Practical Life Skills

School teaches you calculus but rarely teaches you how to file taxes, cook a balanced meal, or maintain a home. These are the practical competencies that affect your daily quality of life — the kind of knowledge that makes you self-sufficient and financially resilient. This file is a dense reference for the essentials.

---

## Personal Finance

### Budgeting: The 50/30/20 Rule

The simplest budgeting framework, popularised by Senator Elizabeth Warren:

| Category | Percentage | Includes |
|----------|-----------|----------|
| **Needs** | 50% | Rent/mortgage, utilities, groceries, insurance, minimum debt payments, transport |
| **Wants** | 30% | Dining out, entertainment, subscriptions, hobbies, travel |
| **Savings & debt repayment** | 20% | Emergency fund, investments, extra debt payments, retirement contributions |

> **Adjust as needed**: In high-cost cities, needs may consume 60-70%. The ratio is a guideline, not a law. The point is to be intentional about where your money goes.

### Key Financial Concepts

| Concept | What It Means | Why It Matters |
|---------|--------------|----------------|
| **Compound interest** | Interest earned on both principal and accumulated interest | $10,000 at 7% annual return = ~$76,000 in 30 years (without adding a cent) |
| **Emergency fund** | 3-6 months of expenses in a liquid savings account | Prevents debt spirals from job loss, medical bills, or car repairs |
| **Credit score** | Numerical rating of your creditworthiness (300-850 in most systems) | Affects mortgage rates, rental applications, insurance premiums |
| **Inflation** | General rise in prices over time; reduces purchasing power | Money saved under a mattress loses ~2-3% of value per year |
| **Diversification** | Spreading investments across different asset types | Reduces risk; never put all your money in one stock or sector |
| **Net worth** | Assets minus liabilities | The real measure of financial health, more than income |

### Debt Management

| Strategy | How It Works | Best For |
|----------|-------------|----------|
| **Avalanche method** | Pay minimums on all debts; put extra toward the highest-interest debt first | Minimises total interest paid; mathematically optimal |
| **Snowball method** | Pay minimums on all debts; put extra toward the smallest balance first | Quick wins build motivation; psychologically effective |
| **Consolidation** | Combine multiple debts into one loan with a lower rate | Simplifies payments; works if you can secure a better rate |

### Tax Basics (General Principles)

- **Income tax**: Most countries tax earnings progressively (higher income = higher rate)
- **Tax-advantaged accounts**: Retirement accounts (401(k), IRA, pension, ISA) reduce tax burden
- **Deductions vs credits**: A deduction reduces taxable income; a credit directly reduces tax owed (credits are more valuable)
- **Common mistakes**: Not filing on time; ignoring freelance/side-income; failing to claim eligible deductions

## Nutrition and Cooking

### Macronutrients

| Nutrient | Calories per Gram | Function | Good Sources |
|----------|-------------------|----------|-------------|
| **Carbohydrates** | 4 kcal | Primary energy source; fuel for brain and muscles | Whole grains, fruits, vegetables, legumes |
| **Protein** | 4 kcal | Building blocks for muscles, enzymes, hormones | Meat, fish, eggs, dairy, legumes, tofu |
| **Fat** | 9 kcal | Dense energy; hormone production; nutrient absorption | Olive oil, nuts, avocado, fatty fish, butter |

### Micronutrients That Matter Most

| Vitamin/Mineral | Function | Deficiency Symptoms | Sources |
|----------------|----------|--------------------|---------|
| **Vitamin D** | Calcium absorption; immune function | Fatigue, bone pain, frequent illness | Sunlight, fatty fish, fortified milk |
| **Iron** | Oxygen transport in blood | Anaemia, fatigue, weakness | Red meat, spinach, lentils, fortified cereals |
| **Vitamin B12** | Nerve function; red blood cell formation | Numbness, fatigue, memory issues | Meat, eggs, dairy; supplement for vegans |
| **Calcium** | Bone and teeth structure; muscle function | Weak bones, muscle cramps | Dairy, leafy greens, fortified plant milk |
| **Magnesium** | 300+ enzyme reactions; sleep; muscle relaxation | Muscle twitches, insomnia, anxiety | Nuts, seeds, dark chocolate, whole grains |
| **Omega-3 fatty acids** | Brain health; anti-inflammatory | Dry skin, poor concentration, joint pain | Fatty fish, flaxseed, walnuts |

### A Balanced Plate

A simple visual guide for every meal:
- **Half the plate**: Vegetables and fruits (variety of colours)
- **Quarter**: Protein (palm-sized portion)
- **Quarter**: Complex carbohydrates (whole grains, starchy vegetables)
- **Add**: Healthy fat (olive oil, nuts, avocado — thumb-sized portion)

### Cooking Fundamentals

| Technique | What It Is | Best For |
|-----------|-----------|----------|
| **Sautéing** | Quick cooking in a small amount of oil over medium-high heat | Vegetables, thin cuts of meat, shrimp |
| **Roasting** | Cooking in an oven at high heat (200-230°C / 400-450°F) | Vegetables, whole chickens, root vegetables |
| **Braising** | Sear first, then slow-cook in liquid at low heat | Tough cuts of meat, stews, flavourful results |
| **Steaming** | Cooking with steam; preserves nutrients and colour | Vegetables, fish, dumplings |
| **Blanching** | Brief boil followed by ice bath | Preserving colour; prepping vegetables for freezing |

### Essential Pantry Staples

| Category | Items |
|----------|-------|
| **Grains** | Rice, pasta, oats, bread (or flour for making it) |
| **Proteins** | Canned beans, canned tuna, eggs, frozen chicken |
| **Vegetables** | Onions, garlic, potatoes, frozen mixed vegetables |
| **Fats** | Olive oil, butter |
| **Seasoning** | Salt, black pepper, soy sauce, vinegar, dried herbs |
| **Dairy/alternatives** | Milk, cheese, yoghurt |

> **Rule of thumb**: If you have onions, garlic, oil, salt, and a protein, you can make something edible. Most recipes are variations on "season and cook."

## Home Maintenance

### Seasonal Checklist

| Season | Tasks |
|--------|-------|
| **Spring** | Clean gutters; check roof for damage; service air conditioning; test smoke detectors; deep clean |
| **Summer** | Mow lawn; check for pest entry points; clean windows; maintain outdoor spaces |
| **Autumn** | Rake leaves; service heating system; check insulation; clean chimney; winterise pipes |
| **Winter** | Prevent frozen pipes (insulate, keep heat on); clear snow; check for drafts; change furnace filters |

### Common Repairs You Can Do Yourself

| Problem | Fix | Tools Needed |
|---------|-----|-------------|
| **Running toilet** | Replace flapper valve (usually the cause) | New flapper ($5); no tools or adjustable wrench |
| **Clogged drain** | Plunger first; then baking soda + vinegar; then drain snake | Plunger, baking soda, vinegar |
| **Drafty windows** | Weatherstripping or caulking around frames | Caulk gun, weatherstripping tape |
| **Squeaky door** | Lubricate hinges with silicone spray or graphite | Spray lubricant |
| **Patched drywall** | Spackle small holes; sand smooth; touch up paint | Spackle, putty knife, sandpaper, paint |
| **Leaky faucet** | Usually a worn washer or O-ring; turn off water first | Adjustable wrench, replacement washers |

### When to Call a Professional

| Situation | Why |
|-----------|-----|
| **Electrical work** (beyond changing a lightbulb) | Fire risk; may require permits |
| **Gas appliances** | Explosion/CO risk; always use a licensed technician |
| **Structural changes** (removing walls, foundation cracks) | Building integrity; permits required |
| **Plumbing behind walls** | Water damage risk if done wrong |
| **Roof repair** | Fall risk; improper repair causes leaks |
| **HVAC system** | Complex; wrong repair can void warranty or cause CO issues |

## Time Management

### The Eisenhower Matrix

| | Urgent | Not Urgent |
|---|---|---|
| **Important** | **Do first** — crises, deadlines, emergencies | **Schedule** — exercise, learning, planning, relationships |
| **Not Important** | **Delegate** — interruptions, some emails, other people's priorities | **Eliminate** — social media scrolling, busywork, time-wasters |

### Practical Techniques

| Technique | How It Works | Best For |
|-----------|-------------|----------|
| **Pomodoro** | 25 minutes focused work → 5 minute break → repeat | Procrastination; maintaining focus through tedious tasks |
| **Time blocking** | Assign specific hours to specific tasks in your calendar | Days with mixed priorities; ensuring important work gets time |
| **2-minute rule** | If a task takes less than 2 minutes, do it immediately | Clearing small tasks that accumulate mentally |
| **Eat the frog** | Do your hardest/most important task first thing in the morning | People who procrastinate on big projects |
| **Batching** | Group similar tasks (emails, calls, errands) into one time block | Reducing context-switching overhead |

### Sleep Fundamentals

| Factor | Recommendation | Why |
|--------|---------------|-----|
| **Duration** | 7-9 hours for adults (most need ~8) | Cognitive function, immune system, emotional regulation |
| **Consistency** | Same bedtime and wake time every day (including weekends) | Regulates circadian rhythm; makes falling asleep easier |
| **Environment** | Cool (18-20°C), dark, quiet | Core body temperature needs to drop for deep sleep |
| **Screens** | No screens 30-60 minutes before bed | Blue light suppresses melatonin production |
| **Caffeine** | None after 2 PM (half-life is ~5-6 hours) | Afternoon coffee is still half-active at bedtime |

## Summary

Practical life skills are not glamorous, but they compound. Learning to cook saves money and improves health. Budgeting prevents debt spirals. Basic home maintenance saves thousands in repair costs. Good sleep and time management multiply your effectiveness in everything else. These are not one-time learnings — they are daily practices that get easier with repetition. Start with one area, build the habit, then move to the next.
