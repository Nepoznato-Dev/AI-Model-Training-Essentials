# World Cultures and Societies

Culture is the framework of a society — the shared beliefs, values, customs, behaviours, and artefacts that members use to cope with their world and with each other. Understanding cultural diversity is not about memorising trivia; it is about recognising that one way of seeing the world is among thousands, and that other systems are internally coherent, historically grounded, and worth understanding on their own terms.

---

## Cultural Dimensions

Anthropologists and cross-cultural psychologists use frameworks to compare societies systematically. The most influential is Geert Hofstede's cultural dimensions model.

| Dimension | What It Measures | Example Contrast |
|-----------|-----------------|-----------------|
| **Power Distance** | How much inequality is accepted as normal | High (Malaysia, Philippines) vs Low (Denmark, New Zealand) |
| **Individualism vs Collectivism** | "I" identity vs "we" identity | High individualism (USA, Australia) vs High collectivism (Guatemala, Indonesia) |
| **Masculinity vs Femininity** | Competition and achievement vs cooperation and quality of life | Masculine (Japan, Hungary) vs Feminine (Sweden, Netherlands) |
| **Uncertainty Avoidance** | Tolerance for ambiguity and unstructured situations | High (Greece, Portugal) vs Low (Singapore, Jamaica) |
| **Long-Term vs Short-Term Orientation** | Future rewards vs present traditions | Long-term (China, South Korea) vs Short-term (USA, Nigeria) |
| **Indulgence vs Restraint** | Freedom to satisfy impulses vs strict social norms | Indulgent (Mexico, Nigeria) vs Restrained (Russia, Egypt) |

> **Practical insight**: These are spectrum positions, not value judgments. A high power-distance culture is not "oppressive" — it may value respect for elders and social harmony. A low power-distance culture is not "chaotic" — it may value meritocracy and open debate.

## Major Cultural Regions

### East Asia

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Countries** | China, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, Vietnam |
| **Philosophical roots** | Confucianism, Taoism, Buddhism, Shinto |
| **Key values** | Filial piety, education, harmony, face (social reputation), collective responsibility |
| **Communication style** | High-context: meaning is implied, indirect, read between the lines |
| **Time orientation** | Long-term; emphasis on persistence and future rewards |
| **Business culture** | Relationship-first; trust built over meals and repeated meetings; hierarchy respected |

East Asian cultures share Chinese writing system influence, chopstick use, and Lunar New Year celebrations, but differ significantly — Japanese communication is far more indirect than Chinese, and Korean business culture is more hierarchical than Taiwanese.

### South Asia

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Countries** | India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Nepal |
| **Philosophical roots** | Hinduism, Buddhism, Islam, Sikhism, Jainism |
| **Key values** | Family duty, respect for elders, spiritual diversity, hospitality (atithi devo bhava — "the guest is god") |
| **Communication style** | High-context; the famous head wobble; indirect disagreement |
| **Social structure** | Extended family systems; historical caste influence in India; strong community bonds |
| **Linguistic diversity** | Hundreds of languages; Hindi, Bengali, Urdu, Tamil, Telugu among the most spoken |

### Middle East and North Africa (MENA)

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Countries** | Saudi Arabia, UAE, Egypt, Morocco, Iran, Turkey, Israel |
| **Philosophical roots** | Islam, Judaism, Christianity (originated here), Ottoman and Persian traditions |
| **Key values** | Hospitality, honour, family loyalty, religious observance, generosity |
| **Communication style** | High-context; relationship-driven; indirect refusal to preserve face |
| **Business culture** | Personal relationships essential; negotiations may be lengthy; Friday is the holy day in Muslim-majority countries |
| **Diversity note** | Enormous range — secular Turkey differs greatly from conservative Saudi Arabia; Lebanon is more cosmopolitan than Yemen |

### Sub-Saharan Africa

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Countries** | 46+ countries; Nigeria, Ethiopia, Kenya, South Africa, Ghana, Senegal among the most populous |
| **Philosophical roots** | Ubuntu ("I am because we are"), ancestral traditions, Islam, Christianity, indigenous belief systems |
| **Key values** | Community, extended family, oral tradition, respect for elders, hospitality |
| **Linguistic diversity** | 2,000+ languages; Swahili, Yoruba, Amharic, Zulu, Hausa, Igbo among the most spoken |
| **Social structure** | Age-grade systems, tribal affiliations, strong communal identity |
| **Diversity note** | The most genetically and culturally diverse continent on Earth. Generalising about "African culture" is like generalising about "European culture" — possible at a high level, but misleading in detail |

### Latin America

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Countries** | Mexico, Brazil, Argentina, Colombia, Peru, Chile, and others |
| **Philosophical roots** | Indigenous civilisations (Aztec, Maya, Inca), Catholicism, African diaspora influence |
| **Key values** | Family (familismo), personal relationships (personalismo), warmth, flexibility with time |
| **Communication style** | High-context; physical proximity and touch are normal; indirect disagreement |
| **Time orientation** | Polychronic — multiple tasks simultaneously; relationships take priority over schedules |
| **Business culture** | Trust and personal connection precede business; meetings start with extended social conversation |

### Western (Europe, North America, Australasia)

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Countries** | USA, Canada, UK, Germany, France, Australia, New Zealand, Nordic countries |
| **Philosophical roots** | Greco-Roman philosophy, Christianity, Enlightenment, liberalism |
| **Key values** | Individualism, meritocracy, directness, punctuality, innovation |
| **Communication style** | Low-context: meaning is explicit; "say what you mean" |
| **Time orientation** | Monochronic — time is a resource to be managed; punctuality is a virtue |
| **Business culture** | Task-first; efficiency valued; contracts over relationships; flat hierarchies (especially in Nordic countries) |

> **Diversity within the West**: Nordic social democracy differs enormously from American capitalism. German directness differs from British politeness. Italian expressiveness differs from Swiss reserve. "Western culture" is a useful shorthand but not a monolith.

## Communication Styles: High-Context vs Low-Context

Edward T. Hall's framework is one of the most practical tools for cross-cultural understanding.

| Feature | High-Context | Low-Context |
|---------|-------------|-------------|
| **Meaning** | In the situation, relationship, and non-verbal cues | In the explicit words |
| **Agreements** | Based on trust; may be verbal | Written contracts preferred |
| **Disagreement** | Indirect; "maybe" often means "no" | Direct; "no" means "no" |
| **Feedback** | Subtle; delivered privately to preserve face | Explicit; delivered directly |
| **Silence** | Comfortable; meaningful | Uncomfortable; something is wrong |
| **Examples** | Japan, China, Arab countries, Latin America | USA, Germany, Scandinavia, Switzerland |

## Social Structures and Organisation

| Structure | Description | Where Common |
|-----------|-------------|-------------|
| **Nuclear family** | Parents + children only | Western countries, urban areas globally |
| **Extended family** | Multiple generations living together or in close contact | South Asia, Middle East, Africa, Latin America |
| **Clan/tribal** | Larger kinship group with shared ancestry | Parts of Africa, Middle East, Central Asia |
| **Caste/class** | Hereditary social stratification | Historical India (now legally abolished); class systems everywhere |
| **Egalitarian** | Minimal hierarchy; merit-based | Nordic countries, online communities |

## Religion and Cultural Practice

Religion is not just belief — it shapes diet, dress, calendar, gender roles, business hours, and legal systems.

| Religion | Adherents | Key Cultural Influences |
|----------|-----------|----------------------|
| **Christianity** | ~2.4 billion | Sunday rest; Christmas/Easter calendar; influenced Western law and ethics |
| **Islam** | ~1.9 billion | Five daily prayers; Ramadan fasting; halal dietary laws; Friday as congregational day |
| **Hinduism** | ~1.2 billion | Caste influence; vegetarianism in many; Diwali/Holi festivals; cow reverence |
| **Buddhism** | ~500 million | Meditation; non-violence; monastic traditions; mindfulness in secular contexts |
| **Sikhism** | ~30 million | Turban; langar (community kitchen); equality; service (seva) |
| **Judaism** | ~15 million | Shabbat (Saturday rest); kosher dietary laws; high holy days; emphasis on study |
| **Secular/non-religious** | ~1.2 billion | No religious calendar; pluralistic values; varies enormously by country |

## Cultural Etiquette Quick Reference

| Region | Greeting | Gift-Giving | Dining | Taboos |
|--------|----------|-------------|--------|--------|
| **Japan** | Bow (depth = respect) | Both hands; unwrap later | Slurp noodles (compliment); remove shoes | Sticking chopsticks upright in rice |
| **Middle East** | Hand on heart; handshake (same gender) | Right hand only; refuse once before accepting | Right hand only for eating | Showing sole of shoe; left hand for food |
| **India** | Namaste (palms together); handshake in cities | Remove wrapping in front of host | Vegetarian common; eat with right hand | Beef (Hindus); pork (Muslims); leather gifts |
| **China** | Slight bow or nod; handshake increasingly common | Refuse twice before accepting; give in pairs | Host pays; leave a little food | Number 4 (sounds like "death"); clock gifts |
| **Western** | Handshake; hug among friends | Open in front of giver | Tip 15-20% (USA); finish your plate | Varies by country |

## Globalisation and Cultural Change

Cultures are not static. Globalisation, migration, and the internet are reshaping cultural practices worldwide.

**Homogenisation vs hybridisation:**
- Some argue globalisation is creating a single consumer culture (McDonald's, Netflix, English as lingua franca)
- Others see hybridisation: local cultures absorb and transform global influences (Bollywood, K-pop, Afrobeat)
- The reality is both: surface-level convergence (clothing, technology) with deep-level diversity (values, communication, identity)

**Key trends:**
- Urbanisation: over 56% of the world's population lives in cities (up from 30% in 1950)
- Migration: ~280 million international migrants worldwide, creating diaspora communities
- Digital culture: social media creates transnational cultural communities
- Language shift: ~40% of the world's ~7,000 languages are endangered; English, Mandarin, and Spanish dominate

## Summary

Understanding other cultures is not about stereotypes — it is about developing cultural intelligence: the ability to recognise that your norms are not universal, to adapt your behaviour appropriately, and to interact respectfully across difference. The frameworks above (cultural dimensions, high/low context, social structures) are tools for thinking, not boxes for labelling people. Every individual is shaped by many cultures simultaneously — national, regional, professional, generational, familial. The goal is not to predict how someone will behave based on their passport, but to approach difference with curiosity rather than judgment.
