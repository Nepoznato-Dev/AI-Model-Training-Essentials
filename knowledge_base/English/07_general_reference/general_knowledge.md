# General Knowledge

This file is a collection of facts and concepts that come up regularly in conversation, news, education, and daily life. Think of it as background knowledge — the kind of thing that helps you understand what you are reading, hearing, or experiencing.

---

## The Solar System

The solar system is the Sun and everything that orbits it: eight planets, dwarf planets, moons, asteroids, comets, and dust.

| Planet | Distance from Sun | Key Feature | Notable Fact |
|--------|------------------|-------------|-------------|
| **Mercury** | 58 million km | Smallest planet; no atmosphere | Temperatures swing from −180°C to 430°C |
| **Venus** | 108 million km | Thick toxic atmosphere | Hottest planet (~465°C) due to runaway greenhouse effect |
| **Earth** | 150 million km | Liquid water; life | Only known planet with life |
| **Mars** | 228 million km | Red (iron oxide surface) | Largest volcano in solar system (Olympus Mons) |
| **Jupiter** | 778 million km | Largest planet | Mass is greater than all other planets combined; Great Red Spot storm |
| **Saturn** | 1.4 billion km | Spectacular ring system | Rings made of ice and rock particles |
| **Uranus** | 2.9 billion km | Rotates on its side | Extreme seasons; each pole gets 42 years of continuous sunlight |
| **Neptune** | 4.5 billion km | Farthest planet | Strongest winds in solar system (~2,100 km/h) |

The Sun is a medium-sized star that converts 600 million tons of hydrogen into helium every second through nuclear fusion. It is about 4.6 billion years old and roughly halfway through its life.

## Earth — The Numbers

| Fact | Value |
|------|-------|
| Age | ~4.5 billion years |
| Diameter | ~12,742 km |
| Surface water | ~71% |
| Atmosphere | ~78% nitrogen, ~21% oxygen, ~1% other |
| Distance to Moon | ~384,400 km |
| Rotation period | ~24 hours (one day) |
| Orbital period | ~365.25 days (one year) |
| Core temperature | ~5,400°C (similar to the Sun's surface) |

The Moon stabilizes Earth's axial tilt, which is why our seasons are relatively stable. Without it, the tilt would wobble chaotically, causing extreme climate shifts.

## Continents and Oceans

### The Seven Continents

| Continent | Area | Population | Key Fact |
|-----------|------|-----------|----------|
| **Asia** | 44.6M km² | ~4.7 billion | Largest and most populous; contains highest point (Everest, 8,849 m) |
| **Africa** | 30.4M km² | ~1.4 billion | Most countries (54); youngest population; Sahara is largest hot desert |
| **North America** | 24.7M km² | ~580 million | Contains Grand Canyon, Great Lakes (largest freshwater system) |
| **South America** | 17.8M km² | ~430 million | Amazon rainforest produces ~6% of world's oxygen; Andes is longest mountain range |
| **Antarctica** | 14.2M km² | ~1,000-5,000 (researchers) | Coldest, driest, windiest; contains 70% of Earth's freshwater (as ice) |
| **Europe** | 10.2M km² | ~750 million | Highest population density; most visited continent for tourism |
| **Australia/Oceania** | 8.5M km² | ~45 million | Smallest continent; unique wildlife (marsupials); Great Barrier Reef |

### The Five Oceans

The Pacific is the largest (covers more area than all land combined). The Atlantic separates the Americas from Europe and Africa. The Indian Ocean is the warmest. The Southern Ocean encircles Antarctica. The Arctic Ocean is the smallest and shallowest.

Average ocean depth: ~3,688 meters. The deepest point is the Mariana Trench at ~11,000 meters — if you dropped Mount Everest in, there would still be over a mile of water above it.

## The Human Body

The human body is a machine of staggering complexity, and most of it runs without any conscious input from you.

| System | Key Numbers | What It Does |
|--------|------------|-------------|
| **Cells** | ~37 trillion | Basic building blocks; different types for different jobs |
| **Bones** | 206 (adult) | Structure, protection, blood cell production |
| **Neurons** | ~86 billion (brain) | Process and transmit information |
| **Blood vessels** | ~100,000 km total | If laid end to end, they would circle Earth 2.5 times |
| **Water content** | ~60% of body weight | Every chemical reaction in your body happens in water |
| **Heart** | ~100,000 beats/day | Pumps ~7,500 liters of blood daily |
| **Lungs** | ~480 million alveoli | Surface area roughly the size of a tennis court |

**Bones**: The longest is the femur (thigh bone). The smallest is the stapes in the ear (3mm). Babies have about 300 bones; many fuse together as you grow.

**Brain**: Uses about 20% of your body's energy despite being only 2% of your weight. It can process visual information in as little as 13 milliseconds.

## Water — The Molecule That Makes Life Possible

Water (H₂O) is strange. It expands when it freezes (which is why ice floats — unusual for a solid). It has a high heat capacity (which is why coastal cities have milder climates). It is an excellent solvent (which is why it carries nutrients through your body).

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| Freezing point | 0°C (32°F) |
| Boiling point | 100°C (212°F) at sea level |
| % of Earth's water that is saltwater | ~97.5% |
| % that is freshwater | ~2.5% (most locked in ice) |
| Accessible freshwater | Less than 1% of all water |

## Light, Speed, and Scale

The speed of light is ~300,000 km/s (186,000 miles/s). This is not just fast — it is the universe's speed limit. Nothing with mass can reach it.

| Distance | How Long Light Takes |
|----------|---------------------|
| Earth to Moon | ~1.3 seconds |
| Sun to Earth | ~8 minutes |
| Sun to Neptune | ~4 hours |
| To nearest star (Proxima Centauri) | ~4.24 years |
| Across the Milky Way | ~100,000 years |
| To nearest galaxy (Andromeda) | ~2.5 million years |

A light-year is a distance, not a time — about 9.46 trillion kilometers.

## Animals — Quick Reference

| Group | Key Feature | Examples | Count |
|-------|------------|----------|-------|
| **Mammals** | Warm-blooded; give live birth; nurse young | Humans, whales, bats, elephants | ~6,400 species |
| **Birds** | Warm-blooded; feathers; lay eggs | Eagles, penguins, hummingbirds | ~10,000 species |
| **Reptiles** | Cold-blooded; scales | Snakes, turtles, crocodiles | ~11,000 species |
| **Amphibians** | Cold-blooded; live on land and water | Frogs, salamanders | ~8,000 species |
| **Fish** | Gills; live in water | Salmon, sharks, clownfish | ~35,000 species |
| **Insects** | Six legs; exoskeleton | Beetles, ants, butterflies | ~1 million+ species |

The blue whale is the largest animal ever known — up to 30 meters long and 200 tons. The tiniest insect is a fairyfly wasp at 0.14mm. Insects outnumber all other animal groups combined.

## Time and Measurement

| Unit | Equals |
|------|--------|
| 1 minute | 60 seconds |
| 1 hour | 60 minutes (3,600 seconds) |
| 1 day | 24 hours |
| 1 week | 7 days |
| 1 year | 365.25 days (hence leap years every 4 years) |
| 1 millennium | 1,000 years |

The Gregorian calendar (the one most of the world uses) was introduced by Pope Gregory XIII in 1582 to fix the drift in the Julian calendar. It is accurate to within about 1 day every 3,030 years.

## Energy Basics

Energy comes in many forms but always obeys one rule: it cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed.

| Source | Type | How It Works |
|--------|------|-------------|
| **Fossil fuels** (oil, coal, gas) | Non-renewable | Burn ancient organic matter; release CO₂ |
| **Nuclear** | Non-renewable (fuel-limited) | Split atoms to release energy; no CO₂ but radioactive waste |
| **Solar** | Renewable | Photons knock electrons loose in silicon |
| **Wind** | Renewable | Moving air spins turbines |
| **Hydroelectric** | Renewable | Falling water spins turbines |
| **Geothermal** | Renewable | Heat from Earth's interior |

Electrical energy is measured in kilowatt-hours (kWh). A typical US household uses about 900 kWh per month.

## Summary

General knowledge is not about memorizing trivia — it is about having enough context to understand the world around you. When you know that the speed of light is the universe's speed limit, you understand why interstellar travel is so difficult. When you know that only 1% of Earth's water is accessible freshwater, you understand why water conflicts are intensifying. When you know how the human body works at a basic level, you can make better decisions about your health. These facts are not isolated — they connect to each other and to everything else.
