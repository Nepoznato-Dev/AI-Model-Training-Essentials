# Environmental Awareness

The environment is not a separate thing "out there" — it is the system that makes everything else possible. Every breath you take, every glass of water you drink, every meal you eat depends on atmospheric composition, water cycles, soil health, and pollinator networks. Understanding how these systems work — and how they are changing — is not optional for an informed person. This file covers the fundamentals of environmental science, the challenges we face, and what sustainability actually means in practice.

---

## Earth's Major Systems

### The Atmosphere

The atmosphere is a thin layer of gases held by gravity — proportionally thinner than the skin on an apple.

| Layer | Altitude | Key Feature |
|-------|----------|-------------|
| **Troposphere** | 0-12 km | Where weather happens; contains ~75% of atmospheric mass |
| **Stratosphere** | 12-50 km | Ozone layer absorbs UV radiation; temperature increases with altitude |
| **Mesosphere** | 50-85 km | Meteors burn up here; coldest layer (~-90°C at top) |
| **Thermosphere** | 85-600 km | Auroras; International Space Station orbits here; very hot but very thin |
| **Exosphere** | 600-10,000 km | Gradual transition to space; atoms escape into vacuum |

**Composition of dry air:**

| Gas | Percentage | Role |
|-----|-----------|------|
| **Nitrogen (N₂)** | 78.09% | Dilutes oxygen; essential for nitrogen cycle |
| **Oxygen (O₂)** | 20.95% | Respiration; combustion |
| **Argon (Ar)** | 0.93% | Inert; used in welding and light bulbs |
| **Carbon dioxide (CO₂)** | ~0.042% (rising) | Greenhouse gas; photosynthesis; climate driver |
| **Trace gases** | <0.01% | Neon, helium, methane, ozone, water vapour |

> **Key insight**: CO₂ is only 0.042% of the atmosphere, but it is the primary climate control knob. Small changes in CO₂ have driven the difference between ice ages and warm periods throughout Earth's history.

## Climate Science Basics

### The Greenhouse Effect

The greenhouse effect is natural and necessary — without it, Earth's average temperature would be about -18°C instead of the current +15°C. The problem is the *enhanced* greenhouse effect caused by additional greenhouse gases.

| Greenhouse Gas | Primary Source | Atmospheric Lifetime | Warming Potential (vs CO₂) |
|---------------|---------------|---------------------|---------------------------|
| **Carbon dioxide (CO₂)** | Fossil fuels, deforestation | 300-1,000 years | 1 (baseline) |
| **Methane (CH₄)** | Agriculture, natural gas leaks, wetlands | ~12 years | ~80 (over 20 years) |
| **Nitrous oxide (N₂O)** | Fertilisers, industrial processes | ~114 years | ~273 |
| **Fluorinated gases** | Refrigerants, industrial | 1,000-50,000 years | 1,000-23,000 |
| **Water vapour** | Evaporation (feedback, not direct driver) | ~9 days | Varies |

### The Evidence for Climate Change

| Evidence | What It Shows |
|----------|--------------|
| **Temperature records** | Global average temperature has risen ~1.2°C since pre-industrial era; accelerating |
| **Ice cores** | CO₂ levels are higher than at any point in 800,000 years |
| **Sea level rise** | ~20 cm since 1900; accelerating (~3.7 mm/year currently) |
| **Arctic sea ice** | September minimum declined ~40% since satellite records began (1979) |
| **Ocean acidification** | Ocean pH has dropped 0.1 units (30% more acidic) since pre-industrial era |
| **Species shifts** | Range shifts toward poles; earlier spring events; coral bleaching |
| **Glacier retreat** | 90%+ of the world's glaciers are retreating |

### Climate Projections

The IPCC uses Shared Socioeconomic Pathways (SSPs) to model futures:

| Scenario | Description | Warming by 2100 |
|----------|-------------|-----------------|
| **SSP1-1.9** | Very aggressive mitigation; net zero by ~2050 | ~1.5°C |
| **SSP1-2.6** | Strong mitigation efforts | ~1.8°C |
| **SSP2-4.5** | Middle of the road; moderate effort | ~2.7°C |
| **SSP3-7.0** | Limited cooperation; high emissions | ~3.6°C |
| **SSP5-8.5** | Fossil-fuelled development; very high emissions | ~4.4°C |

## Ecosystems and Biodiversity

### Major Biomes

| Biome | Climate | Key Characteristics | Biodiversity |
|-------|---------|---------------------|-------------|
| **Tropical rainforest** | Hot, wet year-round | Dense canopy; nutrient-poor soil (nutrients in biomass) | Highest terrestrial biodiversity |
| **Temperate forest** | Moderate; distinct seasons | Deciduous trees; rich soil | Moderate diversity |
| **Grassland/Savanna** | Seasonal rainfall; fire-adapted | Few trees; deep root systems; large herbivores | Moderate diversity |
| **Desert** | Very low precipitation | Adapted plants (succulents); nocturnal animals | Low diversity; specialised species |
| **Tundra** | Very cold; short growing season | Permafrost; low vegetation; migratory birds | Very low diversity |
| **Coral reefs** | Warm, shallow, clear water | Calcium carbonate structures; symbiotic algae | Highest marine biodiversity ("rainforests of the sea") |
| **Wetlands** | Saturated soil; standing water | Water filtration; flood control; carbon storage | High productivity; nursery for fish |

### The Biodiversity Crisis

| Metric | Value | Trend |
|--------|-------|-------|
| **Species described** | ~1.2 million | New species discovered regularly |
| **Estimated total species** | 8-20 million | Most undiscovered; many going extinct before discovery |
| **Vertebrate population decline** | ~69% average decline since 1970 (WWF Living Planet Index) | Accelerating |
| **Insect biomass** | Declining ~1-2% per year in many regions | "Insect apocalypse" |
| **Coral reef loss** | ~50% of coral cover lost since 1950 | Accelerating with warming |
| **Deforestation** | ~10 million hectares/year (mostly tropical) | Slightly slowing but ongoing |
| **Extinction rate** | 100-1,000x background rate | Mass extinction event (6th in Earth's history) |

### Drivers of Biodiversity Loss

| Driver | Mechanism | Example |
|--------|-----------|---------|
| **Habitat destruction** | Land conversion for agriculture, urban development | Amazon deforestation for cattle and soy |
| **Overexploitation** | Harvesting faster than populations can recover | Overfishing; poaching; bushmeat |
| **Pollution** | Chemical contamination of air, water, soil | Pesticides killing pollinators; plastic in oceans |
| **Invasive species** | Non-native species outcompeting natives | Rats on islands; lionfish in the Caribbean; kudzu in the US |
| **Climate change** | Conditions changing faster than species can adapt | Coral bleaching; polar habitat loss; phenological mismatch |

## Sustainability Fundamentals

### The Three Pillars

Sustainability is often described as the intersection of three goals:

| Pillar | Goal | Key Question |
|--------|------|-------------|
| **Environmental** | Protect natural systems | Can the planet sustain this? |
| **Economic** | Maintain prosperity | Is it financially viable long-term? |
| **Social** | Ensure equity and well-being | Is it fair and accessible? |

A practice is only truly sustainable when all three are satisfied. A profitable business that destroys its environment is not sustainable. A conservation program that impoverishes local communities is not sustainable.

### The Circular Economy

The traditional linear economy (extract → make → use → dispose) is being replaced by circular economy principles:

| Linear Economy | Circular Economy |
|---------------|-----------------|
| Take raw materials | Use renewable/recycled inputs |
| Manufacture products | Design for durability and disassembly |
| Use and discard | Reuse, repair, remanufacture |
| Waste to landfill | Waste becomes input for another process |
| Value destroyed | Value maintained and recovered |

### Energy Sources Compared

| Source | Carbon Intensity | Reliability | Land Use | Key Challenge |
|--------|-----------------|-------------|----------|--------------|
| **Coal** | Very high | Dispatchable | Moderate | Emissions; air pollution; mining |
| **Natural gas** | High | Dispatchable | Low-moderate | Methane leaks; still fossil |
| **Nuclear** | Very low | Dispatchable | Very low | Waste disposal; public perception; cost |
| **Solar PV** | Very low (operational) | Intermittent | High | Storage needed; manufacturing impact |
| **Wind** | Very low (operational) | Intermittent | Low (dual use possible) | Visual/noise impact; storage needed |
| **Hydroelectric** | Low | Dispatchable (with reservoir) | Very high | Ecosystem disruption; displacement |
| **Geothermal** | Very low | Dispatchable | Very low | Location-specific |
| **Biomass** | Low-moderate (if sustainable) | Dispatchable | High | Land competition; carbon accounting complex |

### Water: The Critical Resource

| Fact | Value |
|------|-------|
| % of Earth's water that is freshwater | ~2.5% |
| % of freshwater accessible (rivers, lakes, groundwater) | <1% |
| Global agricultural water use | ~70% of all freshwater withdrawals |
| People without safe drinking water | ~2 billion |
| Projected water stress by 2050 | 5+ billion people could face water scarcity |

**Water cycle disruption**: Climate change intensifies the water cycle — wet areas get wetter (more flooding), dry areas get drier (more drought). Mountains that store water as snow are losing that buffer.

## Individual and Systemic Action

### High-Impact Individual Actions

| Action | Annual CO₂ Reduction | Difficulty |
|--------|---------------------|-----------|
| **Avoid one transatlantic flight** | ~1.6 tonnes | Lifestyle change |
| **Eat plant-based diet** | ~0.8-1.5 tonnes | Significant lifestyle change |
| **Switch to renewable energy provider** | ~1.5 tonnes (varies by grid) | Easy (where available) |
| **Drive less / use public transit** | ~1.0-2.5 tonnes | Infrastructure-dependent |
| **Reduce food waste** | ~0.3-0.5 tonnes | Behavioural change |
| **Insulate your home** | ~0.5-1.0 tonnes | Upfront cost; long-term savings |

> **Context**: The global average per-capita carbon footprint is ~4.7 tonnes CO₂e/year. The US average is ~16 tonnes. The target for 1.5°C is ~2.5 tonnes by 2030. Individual actions matter, but systemic change (energy policy, industrial regulation, infrastructure) is essential for reaching scale.

### The Hierarchy of Action

From most to least impactful (for both individuals and organisations):

1. **Refuse** — don't buy what you don't need
2. **Reduce** — consume less of what you do need
3. **Reuse** — extend the life of what you have
4. **Repair** — fix instead of replacing
5. **Recycle** — process materials for new products
6. **Rot** — compost organic waste

## Summary

Environmental awareness is not about doom or guilt — it is about understanding the systems that sustain us. The atmosphere is thin. Biodiversity is declining at rates unprecedented in human history. Freshwater is finite. Climate change is the result of accumulated greenhouse gas emissions, and its worst effects are still avoidable — but the window is narrowing. The good news is that many solutions exist, are cost-competitive, and are scaling rapidly. The question is not whether we have the technology or knowledge, but whether we have the collective will to deploy them at the speed and scale required.
