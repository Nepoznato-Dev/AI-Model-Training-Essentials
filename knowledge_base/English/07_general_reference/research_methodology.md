# Research Methodology

Research methodology is the systematic approach to investigating questions, testing hypotheses, and generating reliable knowledge. Whether you're conducting scientific experiments, social science surveys, business analysis, or academic research, the methodology you choose determines whether your findings are credible, reproducible, and useful. A well-designed study produces trustworthy results; a poorly designed one produces noise — or worse, confident wrong answers.

---

## Types of Research

| Dimension | Options | Description |
|-----------|---------|-------------|
| **Purpose** | Basic vs Applied | Basic: expanding knowledge for its own sake. Applied: solving a practical problem |
| **Approach** | Deductive vs Inductive | Deductive: theory → hypothesis → observation. Inductive: observation → pattern → theory |
| **Data type** | Quantitative vs Qualitative vs Mixed | Quantitative: numbers. Qualitative: words, meanings. Mixed: both |
| **Time horizon** | Cross-sectional vs Longitudinal | Cross-sectional: one point in time. Longitudinal: over time |
| **Setting** | Experimental vs Observational | Experimental: researcher manipulates variables. Observational: researcher observes without intervention |

---

## The Scientific Method

| Step | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **1. Observation** | Notice a phenomenon or pattern |
| **2. Question** | Formulate a specific, testable question |
| **3. Hypothesis** | Propose a tentative explanation or prediction |
| **4. Prediction** | Derive testable predictions from the hypothesis |
| **5. Experiment / Observation** | Collect data to test the predictions |
| **6. Analysis** | Analyse the data; determine if results support or refute the hypothesis |
| **7. Conclusion** | Draw conclusions; consider limitations; suggest further research |
| **8. Peer review and replication** | Others evaluate and attempt to reproduce the findings |

---

## Quantitative Research

### Experimental Designs

| Design | Description | Strength | Weakness |
|--------|-------------|----------|----------|
| **True experiment (RCT)** | Random assignment to treatment and control | Strongest causal evidence | Expensive; may not generalise |
| **Quasi-experiment** | No random assignment; comparison groups | More practical; real-world settings | Confounding variables |
| **Pre-test / post-test** | Measure before and after intervention | Shows change over time | No control for external factors |
| **Factorial design** | Multiple independent variables manipulated simultaneously | Tests interactions between variables | Complex; large sample needed |
| **Within-subjects** | Same participants in all conditions | Controls individual differences; fewer participants | Order effects; carryover |
| **Between-subjects** | Different participants in each condition | No carryover effects | Individual differences; more participants needed |

### Sampling Methods

| Method | Type | Description | When to Use |
|--------|------|-------------|-------------|
| **Simple random** | Probability | Every member has equal chance of selection | Gold standard; needs complete sampling frame |
| **Stratified random** | Probability | Divide into strata; random sample from each | Ensure representation of subgroups |
| **Cluster** | Probability | Randomly select groups; survey all within | Large populations; cost-effective |
| **Convenience** | Non-probability | Sample whoever is available | Quick; cheap; but biased |
| **Purposive** | Non-probability | Deliberately select specific cases | Expert opinions; specific characteristics |
| **Snowball** | Non-probability | Existing participants recruit others | Hard-to-reach populations |

### Statistical Analysis

| Analysis Type | Purpose | Example |
|--------------|---------|---------|
| **Descriptive statistics** | Summarise data | Mean; median; standard deviation; frequency distributions |
| **Inferential statistics** | Draw conclusions about a population from a sample | t-tests; ANOVA; chi-squared; regression |
| **Correlation** | Measure association between variables | Pearson's r; Spearman's rho |
| **Regression** | Model relationship between dependent and independent variables | Linear regression; logistic regression |
| **Factor analysis** | Identify underlying dimensions in data | PCA; exploratory factor analysis |
| **Meta-analysis** | Combine results from multiple studies | Systematic review of treatment effects |

## Qualitative Research

### Methods

| Method | Description | Best For |
|--------|-------------|----------|
| **Interviews** (semi-structured) | Open-ended questions; probing | Deep understanding of experiences; meanings |
| **Focus groups** | Group discussion guided by a moderator | Exploring group norms; generating ideas |
| **Ethnography** | Immersive observation in natural setting | Understanding cultures; social practices |
| **Case study** | In-depth investigation of a single case or small number | Complex phenomena in context |
| **Grounded theory** | Build theory from data through iterative coding | When existing theory is inadequate |
| **Content analysis** | Systematic coding of text/media | Large volumes of text; media analysis |
| **Discourse analysis** | Study of language in use; power relations | How language constructs reality |
| **Phenomenology** | Understanding lived experience | How people experience a phenomenon |

### Ensuring Quality in Qualitative Research

| Criterion | Description | How to Achieve |
|-----------|-------------|----------------|
| **Credibility** (internal validity) | Findings accurately represent participants' views | Triangulation; member checking; prolonged engagement |
| **Transferability** (external validity) | Findings applicable to other contexts | Thick description; purposeful sampling |
| **Dependability** (reliability) | Findings consistent if study were repeated | Audit trail; peer debriefing |
| **Confirmability** (objectivity) | Findings rooted in data, not researcher bias | Reflexivity; triangulation; audit trail |

## Mixed Methods

| Design | Description | When to Use |
|--------|-------------|-------------|
| **Convergent parallel** | Collect quantitative and qualitative data simultaneously; compare | When you want both breadth and depth |
| **Explanatory sequential** | Quantitative first; qualitative to explain results | When survey results need deeper understanding |
| **Exploratory sequential** | Qualitative first; quantitative to test findings | When you need to develop instruments or theory first |

## Validity and Reliability

### Threats to Validity

| Threat | Description | Mitigation |
|--------|-------------|------------|
| **Selection bias** | Groups differ systematically before intervention | Random assignment; matching |
| **Attrition bias** | Participants drop out non-randomly | Track reasons; intention-to-treat analysis |
| **Hawthorne effect** | People change behaviour because they're being observed | Control groups; blinding |
| **Demand characteristics** | Participants guess the hypothesis and act accordingly | Deception; cover stories |
| **Experimenter bias** | Researcher's expectations influence results | Blinding; standardised procedures |
| **Confounding** | Third variable explains the relationship | Control variables; randomisation; statistical adjustment |
| **P-hacking** | Testing many hypotheses until one is significant | Pre-registration; correction for multiple comparisons |

### Reliability Measures

| Measure | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Test-retest** | Same test administered twice; correlation between scores |
| **Inter-rater** | Agreement between different raters / coders |
| **Internal consistency** | Cronbach's alpha; items measuring the same construct |
| **Parallel forms** | Correlation between different versions of the same test |

## Research Ethics

| Principle | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Informed consent** | Participants understand what they're agreeing to; voluntary participation |
| **Beneficence** | Maximise benefits; minimise harm |
| **Non-maleficence** | Do no harm — physical, psychological, social |
| **Justice** | Fair distribution of benefits and burdens; don't exploit vulnerable populations |
| **Privacy and confidentiality** | Protect participants' data; anonymise where possible |
| **Right to withdraw** | Participants can leave the study at any time without penalty |
| **Debriefing** | Explain the study's purpose after participation; especially if deception was used |

## Summary

Research methodology is the framework that determines whether your findings are trustworthy. Quantitative methods (experiments, surveys, statistical analysis) are best for testing hypotheses and measuring effects. Qualitative methods (interviews, ethnography, case studies) are best for understanding meaning, context, and experience. Mixed methods combine both for breadth and depth. The scientific method provides a structured process from observation through hypothesis, data collection, analysis, and peer review. Validity (are you measuring what you think you're measuring?) and reliability (would you get the same results again?) are the key quality criteria. Research ethics ensure that the pursuit of knowledge doesn't come at the expense of participants' rights and wellbeing. The most important principle is that methodology serves the research question — choose the approach that best answers your question, not the one that's most familiar or easiest.
